# Fiction: the story's shape

Read this before rewriting a story. In fiction the sentence-level rules move blind readers
very little: on 30 WritingPrompts replies, assistant stories rewritten with the rest of
this skill were still judged AI in every case (mean P(AI) 75.5 against 7.7 for the human
replies). Across 873 of the judges' reasons, the story's shape carried it: callbacks and
bookends, a clockwork arc, even polish, props placed for texture, a stated moral, a steady
supply of similes. Human replies are looser. Invented detail is the task in fiction, so
these are edits to the story, not only to the wording.

- **Let the ending stay open.** Do not bring the opening image, line or object back in
  the last paragraph, and do not end on a button: a mirrored line, a quip, a resonant
  image. Stop on an ordinary moment, a beat early or late. Leave some planted details
  unpaid.
- **Break the template.** When the story repeats a unit (decades, victims, visits,
  knocks, one character per paragraph), make the units unequal: skip one, merge two, let
  one run long. Do not space the escalation evenly.
- **Uneven pacing.** Let one scene run long and compress another to a sentence. One
  detour that does not serve the twist is fine.
- **No stated moral.** Cut the line that says what the story means, and the aphorism at
  the emotional peak ("She got good at making grief small enough to carry").
- **One or two similes in the whole story**, not one per paragraph. Never follow an image
  with its explanation.
- **Dialogue that misses.** Cut about half the zingers; let a line go unanswered or answer
  the wrong thing. Characters do not explain the rules of the world to each other for the
  reader's benefit.
- **Fewer props.** Keep the one or two specifics that matter to the narrator. Brand names,
  local chains and cute pet names laid down for texture read as placed.
- **Names and angle.** Avoid the stock names (Wren, Okonkwo, Elara, Kael, Marcus, Voss,
  Maya) and, where the prompt allows, the obvious subversion (the weary bureaucrat devil,
  the gentle monster).
- **Hedges only where the narrator doubts.** An even sprinkle of "sort of", "fairly sure"
  reads as de-polishing.
- **Check the facts the story states**: weekdays against dates, ages, who is where.

With these rules the same judges' mean P(AI) fell from 75.2 to 69.3 on those 30 stories
(better on 25) and from 74.0 to 69.7 on 32 stories never used to write them (better on
30, p < 0.001; human replies 6.9). Every rewritten story was still judged AI: what is left
is the premise and the plot the draft chose, which a rewrite keeps.

Keep the fiction band file's other advice: assistant stories already have short, choppy
paragraphs, so do not add fragments for rhythm.
