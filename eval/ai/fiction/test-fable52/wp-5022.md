**Expert**

I woke up on a gray tile, cool and slightly raised, like a key on a giant keyboard. More tiles stretched in every direction, thirty wide and sixteen deep. I counted them later. I counted everything later.

*Beat the game,* said a voice inside my skull, pleasant as a dentist's receptionist, *and you may leave.*

"What game?" I asked, though I already knew. I'd spent my lunch break hunched over it, the same way I'd spent every lunch break since 2009.

Above the horizon, a red digital counter glowed: **099**. Beside it, a yellow face the size of a moon smiled down at me.

Minesweeper. Expert difficulty, because past me was a show-off.

The first click is always safe, and I clung to that as I stepped off my starting tile. The ground behind me sank with a soft *chunk*, and the field ahead cascaded open, dozens of tiles flipping down at once and leaving numbers painted on the floor in cheerful primary colors. There was a blue 1, a green 2, and a red 3 I did not like the look of.

I had always thought of the numbers as a puzzle. Standing on them, I understood them as a census of things nearby that would kill me.

I found the flags in my back pocket, ninety-nine of them, small and red and absurdly light. I planted the first one beside a 1 that touched only a single unopened tile. That was pure logic and nothing could go wrong, but my hand still shook so badly I nearly dropped it.

The counter ticked to **098**. The moon-face kept smiling.

I worked for hours, or maybe days, since the sky never changed. I learned to trust the patterns my fingers had known for years: the 1-2-1, the 1-2-2-1, the corner that gives itself away. At a desk I'd solved those in half a second. Here I checked each one four times and then crawled across the tile on my stomach, as though that would help.

Once, I miscounted. My foot was already descending when I saw it: the 3 was a 3, not a 2, and the tile beneath my shoe was not safe at all. I threw myself backward and lay on a blue 1, wheezing. The moon-face's mouth had become a small, round *o*.

"Don't," I told it. "Don't you make that face at me."

It went back to smiling.

**050**. **031**. **012**. The field filled with flags like a tiny, terrible golf course. I began to feel something I hadn't felt in years of lunch breaks, which was that I was good at this. I had always been good at it, I'd just never had a reason to be proud of that.

**001**.

One flag and two tiles left, side by side in the far corner, walled in by flags and by numbers I'd already used up. I knelt and read every digit around them. Then I read them again, slower, the way you reread a text message hoping it says something different.

A 1 touched both tiles and nothing else did. It was a fifty-fifty.

"No," I said. "No, there's always a logical solution."

*There is not,* said the voice, quite gently. *You know that. You've lost on this exact corner two hundred and eleven times.*

"And how many times have I won it?"

*Two hundred and six.*

I laughed until I had to sit down. Then I sat there a while, on a green 2, in the stillness of a world I'd stared at for fifteen years without ever once looking at it.

The left tile and the right tile were identical. At my desk I always clicked left, with no reason behind it, only a superstition worn smooth by repetition.

I stood up, put the last flag back in my pocket, and looked up at the big yellow face.

"Sunglasses," I told it. "Get them ready."

I stepped right.
