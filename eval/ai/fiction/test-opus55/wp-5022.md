# Prestige

I woke up on a rock.

Not *near* a rock. On one. A smooth gray pebble the size of a parking lot, floating in a pale blue void that smelled faintly of ad revenue.

**WELCOME, PLAYER,** said a voice inside my skull. It had the bright, hollow cheer of a hotel lobby. **TO RETURN HOME, YOU MUST BEAT THE GAME.**

I sat up slowly. Above me, in letters forty feet tall, glowed a number:

**PEBBLES: 0**

And then I remembered. Not the dramatic sci-fi shooter I'd been meaning to finish. Not the hundred-hour fantasy epic gathering dust in my library. No. At 2 a.m., lying in bed, too tired to sleep and too wired to stop, I had opened *Pebble Tycoon*, a free mobile idle game whose entire gameplay loop was *tap the pebble, get a pebble.*

"Voice," I said carefully. "How do I beat *Pebble Tycoon*?"

**YOU MUST BEAT THE GAME.**

"Right, but what's the ending? Is there a boss? A final pebble?"

A long pause. I swear I heard the voice shuffle papers.

**YOU MUST BEAT THE GAME,** it repeated, slightly less confident.

So I tapped the rock.

**PEBBLES: 1**

A small pebble popped out of the ground with a sound like a kazoo being stepped on. I tapped again. And again. By the time my palm was sore, I had four hundred pebbles, and a shimmering shop window had unfolded in the air beside me.

**HIRE A PEBBLE INTERN? COST: 400 PEBBLES.**

I bought it. A small, sad man in a lanyard materialized and began tapping the rock with a hollow expression. He did not speak. I don't think he was allowed to.

Days passed, or maybe hours. There was no sun, only the number. Interns became Pebble Managers. Managers became Pebble Corporations. Somewhere around the second week I unlocked a Pebble Moon, which orbited lazily overhead and produced 3.2 million pebbles per second while making a noise like a dishwasher. The number on the sky climbed into words I didn't recognize: *quattuordecillion, quindecillion, sexdecillion.*

"Is this beating it?" I asked.

**KEEP GOING!** said the voice, which had clearly also never thought about this.

Every so often, a golden pebble would drift across the void. If I caught it, a thirty-second advertisement played directly behind my eyes for a mobile game about merging dragons. I learned to stop catching them.

By month two, the number filled the entire sky and had begun to wrap around the horizon like a scarf. My interns had unionized. The Pebble Moon had developed a Pebble Moon of its own. I sat on my original rock, the first one, now polished smooth from my fingers, and finally understood the thing that should have been obvious at 2 a.m.

Idle games don't end. They're designed not to. That's the whole point. The number goes up so that you'll keep watching the number go up.

There was no final boss.

There was only one thing you could do in *Pebble Tycoon* that felt anything like finishing, a button I had ignored for weeks because it terrified me. It sat at the bottom of the shop, small and red.

**PRESTIGE: RESET ALL PROGRESS. GAIN 1 ANCIENT PEBBLE.**

"Voice," I said. "What happens if I press it?"

**I GENUINELY DO NOT KNOW,** it admitted. It sounded tired too.

I looked at my moon. My interns. My sexdecillions. Two months of my life, if any of this counted as life.

Then I pressed it.

Everything went white, and the number fell silently to zero, and for the first time since I'd arrived, nothing was asking me for anything.

I woke up in bed. My phone was face-down on my chest, battery dead, screen warm. Morning light was coming in through the blinds.

I lay there for a long time, just breathing.

Then I deleted the app.

Somewhere, very faintly, a voice said: **CONGRATULATIONS.**
