# Things I Still Know

The first thing you lose is the word for it.

Not the hunger. The hunger stays. I mean the word for the thing you used to do with your mouth in the mornings, with the hot bitter cup. I stood in my kitchen for three days trying to remember it, holding the cup, and the word came back to me only once, on the fourth day, while I was chewing on the mail carrier.

*Coffee.* That was it. I wrote it on the wall in his blood so I'd have it.

The wall is how I keep track now. My fingers don't work well enough for pens, and paper confuses me, but the wall stays where it is. On the good days I add things. On the bad days I stand in front of it and moan at the shapes until something comes loose.

Here is what the wall says, top to bottom, as of this morning:

**COFFEE.**
**MY NAME IS DANIEL.**
**MARA IS AT HER MOTHER'S. DO NOT GO THERE.**
**THE BUS DOES NOT COME ANYMORE.**
**DON'T EAT THE DOG.**

The dog is gone now. I didn't eat him. He left in the first week, after he figured out what I was, and I think about him more than I think about almost anything. He used to sleep on my feet. I can't feel my feet, but I can feel where he used to be.

---

People imagine it's like being asleep. It isn't. It's more like being very, very drunk at a party where you don't know anyone, and every face you see looks like food. You're aware. You're just not *in charge*. My body does what it wants, and what it mostly wants is to walk toward noise and bite things. I ride along like a passenger in the back of a cab, watching the meter run.

But the body has habits, too. That surprised me. Mine still walks to the bus stop at 7:40 every morning. It stands there with the others, the old woman from 4B and the teenager with the headphones still hanging around his neck. Nobody bites anybody. We just wait. When the bus doesn't come, we shuffle back, and the teenager always goes left at the corner even though he lives right, and nobody corrects him.

I think we're all doing the same thing. Holding on to the last shape our lives had.

---

The hunger is hardest at night. It comes up from somewhere below the stomach, below the body, like it's coming from the ground. It doesn't want meat exactly. It wants *warm*. It wants the thing that's still running in other people, the thing that went out of me in the parking lot of the Kroger when the man in the bloody scrubs grabbed my arm and I thought, stupidly, *he needs help*.

When the hunger comes, I go to the wall and read the third line over and over.

**MARA IS AT HER MOTHER'S. DO NOT GO THERE.**

I wrote that one first, before *coffee*, before my own name. Some part of me knew which things would go fastest and which mattered most. Her mother's house is eleven blocks east. I know because my body has walked six of them, twice, on nights when the hunger was very loud. Both times I turned around. I don't know how. I think the passenger grabbed the wheel.

I don't know if she's alive. I hope she's alive somewhere I can't find.

---

Yesterday a man came through the neighborhood with a rifle and a red bandana over his face. He was careful, checking windows, and he stopped at mine. I was standing at the wall. He watched me for a long time through the glass. I watched him back. I wanted to bite him so badly my jaw clicked.

Then he looked past me, at the writing.

He read it. I saw his eyes go down the lines. When he got to the bottom, to the dog, he made a noise that might have been a laugh or might have been something else. Then he lowered the rifle, put his hand flat on the window for a second, and walked away.

I don't know what that meant. I added it to the wall anyway, as best I could:

**A MAN SAW ME.**

---

I'm losing more every day. This morning I couldn't find the word for the soft thing you put your head on. Pillow, I think. I'm not sure. I'm not sure about Mara's face anymore, either. I know her hair was dark and she laughed with her whole shoulders, but the face itself keeps sliding off when I reach for it.

So here is the last thing I'm going to put on the wall, while I still have the fingers for it. I'm putting it at the bottom, where it'll be the first thing I see when I've forgotten everything above it:

**YOU WERE SOMEBODY. KEEP NOT GOING EAST.**
