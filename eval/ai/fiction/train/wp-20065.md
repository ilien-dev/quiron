# The Duel, Rescheduled

The Crimson Tempest and the Iron Sovereign had sworn, before the gods and the six o'clock news, to fight to the death.

It was meant to happen on a rooftop in downtown Chicago, at midnight, under a full moon. It had been built up for months. They'd been enemies for eleven years. Tempest had destroyed Sovereign's orbital laser. Sovereign had thrown Tempest into Lake Michigan. There was a documentary.

At 11:58 p.m., they faced each other across the gravel roof, capes snapping in the wind.

"At last," said the Iron Sovereign, raising one massive armored fist. "Tonight, it ends."

"Tonight, it ends," agreed the Crimson Tempest, lightning crackling around her hands.

There was a long, dramatic pause.

"Is it me," said Tempest, "or is it really cold?"

Sovereign lowered his fist a little. "It's supposed to get down to nineteen tonight."

"I didn't bring gloves."

"You can shoot lightning."

"It doesn't make you *warm*, Gary. It's not a space heater."

"Don't call me Gary on the roof."

They stood there, shivering, for another minute.

"Next Tuesday?" said Tempest. "It's supposed to be fifty-two."

"Fine," Sovereign said. "Next Tuesday. Same roof."

---

Next Tuesday, it was fifty-two degrees and clear. They met on the roof. They took their positions. Tempest's eyes glowed white. Sovereign's armor hummed with power.

"Now," said Sovereign, "we finish—"

His phone rang.

They both stared at it. It was clipped to his belt, which Tempest had never noticed before.

"You brought a phone to a death duel?"

"It's my mom," Sovereign said, holding up a finger. "Sorry. One second. She never calls this late." He answered it. "Hi, Ma. No, it's fine. No, I'm not busy." Pause. "The *what* is leaking?"

Tempest crossed her arms.

"Okay," Sovereign said into the phone. "Okay, don't touch it. I'll be right there." He hung up. "Water heater."

"Your mom's water heater."

"She's seventy-eight."

Tempest sighed deeply. "Go. Go fix your mother's water heater."

"Thursday?"

"I have book club Thursday."

"You're in a *book club?*"

"We're reading *Middlemarch*. It's very long. Friday."

---

Friday, Tempest was forty minutes late because of a traffic jam on the Dan Ryan, and by the time she arrived, Sovereign had fallen asleep sitting on an air conditioning unit.

She considered killing him right there, in his sleep. It would be easy. He was snoring.

But it seemed cheap. After eleven years, it felt like it should be special.

She poked him with her boot until he woke up.

"I'm sorry," he said, rubbing his helmet. "I was up all night with the water heater. Had to replace the whole thing."

"You could have texted."

"I don't have your number."

This seemed like an oversight, so they exchanged numbers.

---

After that, it got worse.

The next attempt was postponed because Tempest had a dentist appointment. The one after that was called off because the roof had been booked for a wedding. ("We could just fight at the wedding," said Sovereign. "Absolutely not," said Tempest. "They paid for that roof.")

They agreed on a Sunday, but Sovereign had a cold. He showed up anyway, sneezing inside his helmet, which echoed.

"I can't kill you like this," Tempest said, horrified. "It'd be like beating a puppy."

"I'm *fine*," Sovereign said, and sneezed so hard his visor popped open.

She brought him soup the next day. It was just practical. She wanted him at full strength.

---

It went on for months.

They rescheduled for a Bears game. They rescheduled for Sovereign's nephew's bar mitzvah, which, it turned out, Tempest also attended, because the nephew had asked for her autograph and it seemed rude to refuse. They rescheduled when Tempest's cat got sick. Sovereign sent a card. It had a drawing of a cat on it. He'd drawn it himself. It was very bad.

They started texting. At first just logistics. *Roof at 12?* *Can't, dinner w/ sister. 1?* Then other things. A photo of a weird cloud. A link to an article about a new villain in Detroit that they both agreed was derivative. Sovereign complaining about his armor's warranty. Tempest complaining about *Middlemarch*.

---

On the first anniversary of the original duel, they met on the roof at midnight. Full moon. Cold wind. Capes snapping.

They faced each other.

"At last," said the Iron Sovereign, raising his fist.

"At last," said the Crimson Tempest, lightning crackling.

The long, dramatic pause stretched on. And on.

"Gary," she said finally.

"I know."

"Are we ever actually going to do this?"

He lowered his fist. He looked out over the city, at all the lights, at the lake beyond it.

"Probably not," he admitted.

She let the lightning die in her hands.

"There's a diner on Clark Street that's open all night," she said. "They have pie."

"What kind of pie?"

"All the kinds, Gary."

He considered this with the gravity of a man who had once tried to hold the city hostage with a weather machine.

"We'll fight after pie," he said.

"Sure," she said. "After pie."

They did not fight after pie. They split the check.
