# Letter from 2247

Dear ancestors,

I'm writing this in 2247, which means you're reading it in 2024 or so. I know this seems impossible. Probability-wise it probably is. But here's what I need you to understand about your time from where I'm sitting: everything you're doing is unspeakable.

Let me clarify. I don't mean morally unspeakable in the sense that you're committing atrocities—though by modern standards, you are. I mean unspeakable in the sense that you've normalized cruelty to a degree that we literally cannot speak about your practices without extreme emotional distress. Historians require psychological counseling after deep study of your era.

Your concept of "monogamy" is a form of indentured servitude that we find absolutely barbaric. The idea that two people sign a contract agreeing to restrict their emotional bonds exclusively to each other, in perpetuity, regardless of growth or change—it reads like sexual slavery. The entire institution is built on the assumption that romantic love should supersede personal autonomy, and the casual way you treat it as normal is genuinely disturbing.

Your family structure is frankly horrifying. You assign children to biological parents and expect them to remain in that assignment for eighteen years, removing them from education systems and community support to be raised by two or three overwhelmed adults with zero formal training in childhood development. When parents are inadequate—which they frequently are—children suffer, but somehow this remains legal everywhere. You've just accepted that childraising should be a private matter involving minimal oversight. That alone would be grounds for historical prosecution.

Your concept of "employment" is another thing I barely have words for. You sell your time and labor to other humans for money you need to survive. You call it freedom because you technically have choices between employers, but that's like saying prisoners are free because they can choose which cell to sit in. You spend most of your waking hours doing things others demand of you, under threat of poverty. That you see this as normal is the part that makes historians truly sick.

The way you treat your bodies is incomprehensible to us. You damage them deliberately through environmental toxins, intentionally ingest addictive substances, and deny them basic rest and movement. Then you treat the resulting illness as individual moral failure rather than systemic cruelty. The acceptance of sleep deprivation as a sign of dedication rather than abuse—it's like watching people celebrate self-harm.

Your relationship with food is genuinely disturbing. You've engineered plants and animals specifically to maximize caloric density and addictive qualities while minimizing nutritional value, then you shame people whose bodies respond to this manipulation. You've created a system where basic nutrition is a luxury good that many can't afford. The casual cruelty with which you discuss people's bodies is something we've had to age-restrict in history courses.

Your concept of "nationality" and "citizenship" involves literally drawing lines and declaring that some humans have rights based on where they happened to be born. You've built military systems to enforce these arbitrary divisions, and most of you were taught to find this reasonable. We find it shockingly primitive.

Your education system requires children to sit still for eight hours learning things decided by distant committees, removing them from practical knowledge and authentic community contribution. You measure their worth through standardized testing. The suicide rates among your young people should have triggered systematic reform, but instead you've largely accepted it as inevitable.

The most unspeakable part might be that you *know* these things are wrong. People in your time argue about them constantly. But the systems are so entangled with how you've organized society that you feel trapped. You are trapped, actually. But rather than admitting the system itself is the problem, you've developed elaborate narratives about personal responsibility and market forces and human nature.

That's what makes it unspeakable. It's not ignorance. It's willful maintenance of cruelty while knowing better.

By our era, most of these practices have been reformed. Childraising is a community responsibility. Employment is voluntary contribution to systems you help design. Monogamy is one option among many relationship structures, not the default. Bodies are supported rather than shamed. Education is individualized and community-integrated.

I'm not claiming we're perfect. We have new atrocities I'm sure people reading this in 2400 will find equally barbaric. But we stopped pretending that your practices were acceptable somewhere around 2140, when digital records finally made the scale of harm undeniable.

I'm sorry you lived in that world. I'm sorry you couldn't quite fix it. But I'm telling you now, in case it matters: future people do look back at you with compassion. We know it wasn't easy. We know the systems had weight. We know many of you fought against them.

But we also know you were barbaric.

I hope that helps somehow.

Your descendant,
Diana Chen (2247)
