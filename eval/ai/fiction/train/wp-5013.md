# No Exceptions

Officer Dana Kowalski had been with the Bureau of Rule 34 Enforcement for eleven years, and she had seen things.

She didn't talk about them at parties. Nobody at the Bureau did. When people asked what she did for a living, she said "compliance," which was technically true and made their eyes glaze over immediately.

The Bureau's mandate was simple, and it was carved over the front entrance of the building in stern capital letters:

IF IT EXISTS, THERE IS PORN OF IT. NO EXCEPTIONS.

Her job was to find the exceptions. And then, through a network of contracted artists, writers, and extremely tired freelancers, to make them not exceptions anymore.

---

Monday morning, her supervisor dropped a file on her desk.

"New one," said Captain Reyes. He was sixty and had the thousand-yard stare of a man who had personally overseen the Great Mobile Game Backlog of 2019. "Came in over the weekend."

Dana opened the file. Inside was a single printed photo.

"It's a stapler," she said.

"It's a *new* stapler. Office Depot released it Friday. The Swingline Ergo-Flex 3000." Reyes rubbed his eyes. "Internet sweep turned up nothing. Zero results. It's been out seventy-two hours."

Dana sighed. "Anthropomorphized?"

"Try it straight first. Department's been trying to cut down on anthropomorphization. Headquarters says it's a crutch."

"It's a *stapler*, Captain."

"No exceptions, Kowalski."

---

She called her artists. Most of them didn't pick up. The one who did, a guy named Marcus who worked out of a studio apartment in Portland and billed the government for about forty hours a week, listened to the brief in silence.

"The stapler," he said.

"The Ergo-Flex 3000."

"Is there, like, a character? A mascot?"

"There's no mascot, Marcus."

A long pause. "Can it be two staplers?"

"I'll allow two staplers."

---

By Wednesday, the stapler was handled. Marcus had delivered something tasteful, if you could call it that, involving the Ergo-Flex and a vintage three-hole punch, and it had been uploaded to the appropriate archives with the appropriate tags. Dana closed the file and felt the brief, hollow satisfaction of a job done.

Then Reyes dropped the next file on her desk.

She opened it. She looked at it for a long time.

"This is a picture of the Bureau," she said.

"New building," said Reyes. "We moved in two weeks ago. Somebody in the Philadelphia office flagged it."

"You want me to commission porn of *our own office building?*"

"If it exists," Reyes said wearily, pointing at the ceiling, toward the letters carved over the door.

Dana leaned back in her chair. She looked out the window at the parking lot. She thought about her pension, and how far away it was.

"Captain," she said. "Can I ask you something?"

"Go ahead."

"Has anyone ever just... let one go? One thing, in the whole world, that nobody made porn of?"

Reyes was quiet for a moment. He looked older than usual.

"There was a guy before your time," he said. "Hendricks. Found a species of deep-sea snail nobody'd ever done. Just one snail. He decided to leave it alone. Said the world should have one thing that was clean."

"What happened to him?"

"Somebody on DeviantArt found it within the week." Reyes shook his head. "Didn't even need us. The snail never stood a chance."

He tapped the file.

"That's the thing, Kowalski. We're not really the ones doing it. We're just making sure. The internet always gets there eventually. We're just here to speed things up."

He left. Dana looked at the photo of the building for a long, long time.

Then she picked up the phone and called Marcus.

"Can it be two buildings?" he asked.
