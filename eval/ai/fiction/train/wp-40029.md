# Speak Now

It started during the processional.

I was standing at the altar in my rented tux, watching Claire walk toward me down the aisle on her father's arm, and I felt something cold slide into the back of my skull. Like a draft. Like someone had opened a door inside my head.

I blinked. The feeling passed.

Claire was beautiful. Her dress was simple, ivory, and she was trying very hard not to cry and failing. Her father looked like he was about to pass out. My best man, Devon, nudged me with his elbow and whispered, "Breathe, dude."

I breathed.

*Hello,* said a voice in my head.

---

It wasn't my voice. It was older, and it was dry, like paper rubbing together, and it seemed very pleased with itself.

*Don't be alarmed,* it said. *Well. Be alarmed. It doesn't matter much.*

Claire reached the altar. Her father kissed her cheek and put her hand in mine. Her fingers were warm. Mine, I realized, had gone ice cold.

"Dearly beloved," began Father Martinez.

*Your family bought this land,* said the voice, *in 1894. From a man who did not want to sell it. He made a promise before he died. I am that promise. I have been waiting a very long time for a Callahan to be at his happiest.*

I tried to say something. Anything. My mouth didn't move.

*There we are,* said the voice.

---

"We are gathered here today," said Father Martinez, "to join this man and this woman—"

My right hand twitched in Claire's. She glanced down, then up at me, and smiled nervously. I could feel the spirit moving through me like ink through water, spreading slowly outward from my spine.

I pushed back. I didn't know how, exactly. I just thought, very hard, about Claire. About the day we met, at the dog park, when her terrier stole my sandwich. About our first apartment, with the radiator that clanked all night. About her laugh.

My hand stopped twitching.

*Oh,* said the voice, a little surprised. *That's cute.*

---

The readings went by. Claire's sister read Corinthians, and her voice shook on *love is patient, love is kind*, and I felt the spirit gather itself somewhere behind my eyes.

It went for my left leg this time. I felt it lock at the knee. I swayed. Devon grabbed my elbow.

"You okay, man?" he whispered.

*Tell him you're fine,* said the voice. *Or tell him something else. Tell him you've never loved her. Tell him you're only here for her father's money.*

My mouth opened.

"I'm—" I said, and it was my voice, but it wasn't my words coming up behind it. I could feel the other ones rising in my throat like bile. I clamped my jaw shut so hard my teeth ached.

"Just nervous," I managed.

Devon patted my back. The spirit hissed.

---

"The couple has written their own vows," said Father Martinez.

Oh no.

Claire went first. She unfolded a piece of paper with trembling hands. She talked about the sandwich, and the radiator, and the time I drove four hours in a snowstorm to bring her soup when she had the flu. She said she didn't believe in fate but she believed in me. By the end, half the church was sniffling.

Then everyone looked at me.

*My turn,* said the voice.

I felt it surge. My whole body went cold. My vision tunneled. I felt my own mouth begin to shape a word, a cruel one, and I knew, suddenly, exactly what it wanted: not to hurt me, but to hurt *her*. To say something here, in front of everyone, that she would never forget. To ruin the happiest day of a Callahan's life, the way someone had once ruined his.

I thought about the soup. The snowstorm. The four hours of white road and my hands on the wheel, and her face when she opened the door.

And I held on.

"Claire," I said. My voice cracked, and it was mine. "I wrote something, but I can't read it. I can't— I'm having a hard time right now. So I'm just going to say this."

The spirit clawed at the inside of my ribs.

"There's a part of me," I said, "that's scared. That's angry. That's old and bitter and wants to wreck everything good, just because it can."

Some of the guests shifted uncomfortably. Claire's father frowned.

"And I'm choosing you over it," I said. "Today. And every day after this. I'm choosing you."

---

There was a silence in my head. Then, very faintly:

*Hm.*

The cold began to drain out of my arms, my legs, my chest. It didn't leave, exactly. I could still feel it, somewhere way down deep, sulking.

*This isn't over,* it said.

*I know,* I thought back. *But I'm going to be really, really happy. For a really long time. Good luck with that.*

"You may kiss the bride," said Father Martinez.

I did.

In the very back of my head, something old and bitter sighed like a man settling in for a long wait.

Claire pulled back and looked at me, eyes wet. "That was a weird vow," she whispered.

"I'll explain later," I said.

"Is it a good story?"

"It's a family story," I said. "You'll love it."
