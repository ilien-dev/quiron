By the time Sir Aldric reached the tower, three villages had warned him about the dragon’s fire.

“Burned Lord Pelham alive,” said a miller.

“Roasted six challengers in one evening,” whispered an innkeeper.

“Absolutely murdered my cousin,” said a boy, who seemed suspiciously pleased about it.

Aldric tightened his grip on his sword.

At the tower, he found a queue.

Young men in velvet cloaks waited outside a wooden door, clutching scraps of parchment. From within came a steady thumping, accompanied by the unmistakable sound of a crowd enjoying somebody else’s humiliation.

Then a voice boomed:

“Your father bought your knighthood, bought your horse, bought all your gear—
Only thing you earned yourself’s the right to disappear.”

The crowd erupted.

A man stumbled through the door, pale and trembling.

Lord Pelham.

“You’re alive,” Aldric said.

“Technically.”

Pelham pushed past him.

Aldric entered.

The chamber had been converted into a tavern without the courtesy of serving drinks. Candles lined a makeshift platform. A broad man in a red coat stood at its center, wearing enough gold to destabilize a small kingdom.

Behind him hung a banner:

**DRAGON’S DEN: BRING BARS OR GET BURNED.**

At the back, Princess Elspeth sat behind a desk stacked with coins.

Aldric drew his sword.

“I have come for the princess!”

The room went silent.

The man in red looked him over.

“Course you have. Shiny hat, massive knife. Nobody ever comes for the poetry.”

“Are you the dragon?”

“Just Dragon. No ‘the.’”

“Where are your wings?”

“Where’s your personality?”

Several people made a prolonged, wounded noise.

Aldric raised his blade.

“Release Her Highness.”

Elspeth stood.

“Oh, thank goodness. Did Father send the locksmith?”

Aldric lowered his sword slightly.

“The what?”

“The locksmith. The upper door has been jammed for eleven days. I sent three letters.”

“The king said you were imprisoned.”

“I am. Upstairs. When I’m upstairs.”

“You’re downstairs now.”

“Yes. Dragon built a ladder outside the window. Do keep up.”

Dragon folded his arms.

“Her dad heard ‘locked in a tower with Dragon’ and stopped reading.”

Aldric considered the month he had spent crossing swamps.

“The screaming?”

“Open auditions,” said Elspeth.

“The smoke?”

“Atmosphere,” said Dragon.

“The scorched bodies?”

There was an uncomfortable pause.

“Kitchen accident,” Elspeth said. “We’ve dismissed the cook.”

Aldric sheathed his sword.

“Then I shall escort you home.”

“No.”

“Your Highness—”

“I’m earning money. Actual money. Father thinks a royal budget is something one announces before borrowing from the abbey.”

She gestured at Dragon.

“He performs. I manage bookings. We split the door.”

“You consort with this… this rhyming merchant?”

Dragon smiled.

“Oh, he wants a round.”

The crowd began thumping the tables.

Aldric stepped back.

“I want nothing of the sort.”

“Then apologize,” said Elspeth.

Aldric looked at Dragon’s gold chains, then at the grinning faces around him. He had been laughed at in finer halls than this. Usually by men whose horses he had saddled.

“I have ridden thirty days,” he said. “I would prefer to do what I came for.”

Dragon’s smile faded a little.

“And she’s told you she’s staying.”

Aldric looked toward the princess. She waited, arms folded.

He knew that expression. His sister had worn it when he tried to forbid her marriage to a cooper.

With a sigh, he unbuckled his sword and handed it to Elspeth.

“One round.”

Dragon went first.

He mocked Aldric’s dented helmet, his muddy boots, his heroic posture. He compared his beard to a distressed hedgehog. The audience howled.

Aldric stood motionless.

He had faced cavalry charges. This was worse. The cavalry had never noticed his ears.

Then Dragon extended a hand.

“Your turn, Sir Rescue.”

Aldric searched his mind.

Nothing.

He looked at Elspeth, who gave him an encouraging nod.

He looked at Dragon.

At the chains.

At the banner.

Finally, he spoke.

“You call yourself Dragon. I expected something grand.
But you’re charging village children just to watch you wave your hand.”

A few people laughed.

Dragon nodded appreciatively.

Aldric took a breath.

“You boast of burning nobles. Well, I’ve heard what you can do.
Yet the princess does your counting, and your washing’s hanging too.”

The room exploded.

Dragon glanced toward a row of enormous red underpants drying by the hearth.

Elspeth bent over her ledger, shoulders shaking.

Aldric smiled for the first time in thirty days.

“Another?” he asked.

“Absolutely,” said Dragon.

They sent the king a letter that evening.

Elspeth explained the misunderstanding and requested a locksmith.

Aldric added that the princess was safe, the dragon was manageable, and he would require another fortnight.

He needed time to work on his second verse.
