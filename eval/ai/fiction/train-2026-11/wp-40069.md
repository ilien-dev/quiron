The first rule of robbing Reddit is never tell Reddit you’re robbing Reddit.

The second rule is that someone will figure it out anyway.

“We need a distraction,” said Finch.

Outside our safehouse, an orange train carried another load of karma toward the Capital. Through the floorboards, I could hear the downvotes scratching at the foundations.

“How big?”

“Front page.”

I looked at our crew: a Tumblr forger, a Facebook expatriate, and a woman from LinkedIn who claimed to be a visionary but mostly stole staplers.

“We’re going to need a cat.”

***

Karma wasn’t stored in banks. That would have made sense, and Reddit had outlawed making sense during the Great Redesign.

Instead, it lived in the Vault of Collective Approval, beneath a statue of a man correcting another man about something neither understood.

Every upvote in the country passed through the Vault before reaching its owner.

For eight seconds every midnight, the auditors reconciled the ledgers.

For eight seconds, karma belonged to nobody.

Finch intended to change that.

Our forger, Moth, manufactured credentials identifying us as moderators of an obscure agricultural district.

“r/TurnipExchange?” I asked.

“Real place. Terrifying politics.”

Denise, our LinkedIn operative, studied the documents.

“My title should say Senior.”

“It says Assistant Turnip Inspector.”

“I have eleven years of experience.”

“In turnips?”

“In leadership.”

We compromised on Senior Assistant Turnip Inspector.

***

At eleven fifty-three, our distraction went live.

It was a photograph of a cat sitting in a tiny wheelchair beneath the headline: HE REFUSES TO GIVE UP.

The wheelchair was real. The cat was fine. He simply enjoyed sitting.

At eleven fifty-four, the post crossed the border from Aww into Popular.

At eleven fifty-five, somebody accused the cat of being a repost.

At eleven fifty-six, somebody else discovered that the wheelchair manufacturer had once donated to a controversial mayor.

By eleven fifty-seven, six districts were burning.

“Beautiful,” whispered Finch.

We entered through the service gate.

The guard examined our credentials.

“Turnips?”

“Emergency inspection,” I said.

He leaned closer.

“Actually, turnips are technically—”

Moth hit him with a sack of commemorative Notes.

We dragged him behind a vending machine selling awards nobody could afford.

***

The Vault was smaller than I expected.

Millions of orange arrows drifted behind glass, rising and falling like sparks above a bonfire. Some were barely visible. Others blazed around ancient posts: the broken arms, the coconut, the man who also chose that guy’s wife.

Finch connected his equipment.

Moth watched the corridor.

Denise took a stapler off the auditor’s desk.

“Seriously?”

“Culture fit,” she said.

Then the glass lit red.

A voice spoke from the ceiling.

“We did it, Reddit.”

Finch went still.

The Vault door slammed.

On the wall, a screen displayed our cat.

Below it, a new comment:

*Wheelchair shadows don’t match. Something’s up.*

Another:

*OP moderates r/TurnipExchange.*

Another:

*Why are three turnip inspectors entering the national karma reserve?*

“They found us,” said Moth.

“Of course they found us,” Finch snapped. “They identified a guy once from his ceiling.”

The countdown began.

EIGHT.

Finch reached for the transfer switch.

I caught his wrist.

“What are you doing?”

I looked at the screen, where strangers were assembling our lives from photographs, timestamps, and one unfortunate comment Denise had made about workplace sandwiches.

I knew what they wanted.

“Opening an account,” I said.

SEVEN.

I posted:

*I’m currently robbing the Karma Vault. AMA.*

***

They asked about our equipment.

Our childhoods.

Whether the guard was okay.

Whether we would rather fight one horse-sized moderator or a hundred moderator-sized horses.

I answered everything.

Finch called me an idiot. Moth started laughing.

Then the upvotes came.

Enough to flood the account. Enough to trigger an automatic withdrawal through the emergency payout system.

At midnight, the Vault transferred twelve million karma directly to me.

Legally.

Mostly.

We escaped through the awards chute.

***

I retired somewhere quiet, near the border with Wikipedia.

Denise founded a consultancy. Moth married the guard.

Finch still won’t speak to me.

He says it wasn’t a heist.

He says we just made content.

Some nights, that keeps me awake.
