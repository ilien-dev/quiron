# The Appointed Hour

The duel had been scheduled for dawn on the cliffs of Harrowmere, as these things always are.

Mara Venn arrived first, her storm-blade humming at her hip, her cloak snapping dramatically in the wind she had arranged specifically for the occasion. She had practiced her opening line for eleven years: *"For my father, for my village, for every ash-choked breath you stole."*

Teodric Vale crested the ridge a minute later, wreathed in black fire, eyes like twin furnaces. He stopped thirty paces away and planted his greatsword in the stone.

"Venn."

"Vale."

The wind howled. Somewhere a hawk screamed, right on cue.

"Before we begin," Teodric said, "is that a new cloak?"

Mara blinked. "What?"

"The cloak. It's nice. Very dramatic. I just think it'd be a shame to get it bloodied on its first outing."

She looked down at it. It *was* new. She'd had it made in Corvane, double-lined, silver thread along the hem. It had cost her three months of bounty work.

"It is a very good cloak," she admitted.

"You should break it in first. Wear it around a bit. Let it soften."

They agreed to reconvene in a week.

---

The second duel was set for the Shattered Plains at high noon.

This time Mara arrived in her old cloak. Teodric arrived limping.

"You're injured," she said flatly.

"Turned my ankle on the stairs."

"You can command the fires of the underworld."

"The fires of the underworld don't do anything for stairs, Venn. Have you *seen* the stairs in my fortress? Spiral. No railing. Who designed that?"

"You did. You murdered the architect."

"Well, he should've put in a railing." He winced and shifted his weight. "Look, I'm not going to fight you at eighty percent. You deserve better than that. Your father deserves better than that."

It was, annoyingly, a very honorable thing to say.

"Two weeks," she said.

"Two weeks."

---

Two weeks later, at the Ruins of Old Kesh, it was Mara who had to call it off.

"My sister's getting married," she explained.

"Today?"

"Next month. But she's asked me to help with the seating chart, and if I die here, Aunt Delphine ends up next to Cousin Brannoch, and the last time that happened there was a stabbing."

Teodric considered this with the gravity of a man who had personally overseen several stabbings. "You can't put Delphine next to Brannoch."

"I *know* I can't."

"Have you tried putting the children's table between them? As a buffer?"

She stared at him. "That's... actually not bad."

"I've arranged a lot of war councils. Same basic principle." He hefted his sword onto his shoulder. "After the wedding, then."

"After the wedding."

---

After the wedding, Teodric's dark god was having a festival.

After the festival, Mara's storm-blade went in for resharpening, and the smith in Corvane had a backlog.

After the resharpening, there was a lunar eclipse, and they both agreed it would be visually distracting.

After the eclipse, Teodric got a cat.

"You got a *cat*," Mara said. They were standing on the Bridge of Sorrows, which neither of them had particularly wanted to fight on in the first place, because it was narrow and the railings were low and frankly, after the ankle incident, Teodric was gun-shy about anything structurally questionable.

"She showed up at the fortress. Skinny little thing. One ear." He held up his gauntleted hand, and Mara saw the scratches. "She doesn't like me yet. But she will. And if I die, who's going to feed her? The skeleton guards? They don't even know what a cat *is*."

"You could leave instructions."

"For *skeletons*, Venn?"

Mara opened her mouth, then closed it.

"What's her name?" she asked eventually.

"Ember."

"That's very on-brand for you."

"I'm aware."

---

Eight months after the original appointment, they met again at the cliffs of Harrowmere, back where it began. The hawk was there. The wind was there. Mara was wearing the cloak, which had softened beautifully.

Neither of them drew a weapon.

"I've got nothing," Teodric said finally. "No festival. Ankle's fine. Ember's eating out of my hand. I've got no excuse."

"Neither do I." Mara looked out at the sea. "The seating chart worked, by the way. Nobody got stabbed. Delphine and Brannoch didn't even look at each other."

"The children's table."

"The children's table."

The silence stretched. Below them, waves broke white against the rocks.

"I did burn your village," Teodric said quietly. "I want you to know I don't pretend otherwise."

"I know."

"Your father fought well. Better than any of the others. He nearly had me."

Mara's hand tightened on the hilt of her blade. For a long moment, the old fury rose in her, eleven years of it, hot and bright and clean.

And then it settled, the way a storm settles. Not gone. Just tired.

"You know what my father would've said if he'd seen the last eight months?" she asked.

"What?"

"He'd have said, *Mara, you idiot, you've spent more time planning this man's death than you ever spent living your own life.*" She let go of the sword. "And then he'd have asked about the cat."

Teodric was very still.

"So what happens now?" he said.

"Now?" Mara pulled her cloak tighter against the wind. "Now I think we reschedule."

"To when?"

"I don't know yet. I'll check my calendar." She turned toward the path down the cliffs, then paused. "Bring Ember next time. I want to see this one-eared monster that tamed the Scourge of the Eastern Reach."

Behind her, the Scourge of the Eastern Reach laughed for the first time in eleven years.

"She's going to hate you," he called.

"Everyone does, at first," Mara said, and kept walking.
