**The Last Appointment**

Vess Orlanna had killed four hundred and twelve people across nineteen worlds, and not one of them had seen it coming. That was the craft. A target should die in the middle of an ordinary thought.

The contract for Maren Tolliver came through a dead-drop in the Kessel Arcology, with a payment large enough that Vess read the number twice.

*Subject has survived: a hull breach, two orbital bombardments, a plague quarantine, an assassination attempt by the Veil Syndicate, and an assassination attempt by the Veil Syndicate's replacement.*

"Bad luck," Vess muttered. "Luck runs out."

---

The first attempt was clean. A neurotoxin in the water supply of Maren's habitat, calibrated to her body mass.

Maren, it turned out, had been born on a world with poisoned wells and had carried a filter-straw around her neck since childhood. She drank from the tap, frowned, and fished the straw out of her collar. The rest of the building got sick. She did not.

The second attempt was a sniper's shot from nine kilometers, through a window, in a windless hour.

Maren dropped her cup. She bent to pick it up. The bullet passed through the space where her head had been and killed a ceramic vase instead. She looked at the vase for a long moment, then went back to her breakfast.

The third was a bomb under her transport. The transport was late. The fourth was a poisoned dart. She sneezed.

By the seventh attempt, Vess was no longer angry. She was something closer to reverent.

---

She stopped hunting and started watching. For eleven days she sat across plazas and rode the same trams, studying Maren Tolliver the way a pilgrim studies a shrine.

There was no trick. That was what unsettled her. Maren was not lucky, not enhanced, not guarded. She was simply a woman who refused, at every moment, to be finished. She checked the locks twice. She kept water in her bag. She walked the long way home. She had a hundred small habits, and each one was a door she'd quietly closed on death before it knocked.

On the twelfth day, Maren looked directly at Vess across a crowded market and raised two fingers in greeting.

---

Vess found her seat at a noodle stall. Maren slid a bowl across the counter without being asked.

"You're the one with the vase," Maren said. "I liked that vase."

Vess said nothing. Her hands stayed visible, which was the only courtesy she could think of.

"Seven tries," Maren said. "Most people give up at three. The Veil people gave up at five and sent flowers, which I thought was classy." She slurped her noodles. "Who's paying you?"

"A man named Corvane. You testified against him on Dessa Prime."

"Ah. That." Maren nodded slowly. "He'll keep sending people, you know. After you."

"I know."

"So you've got a problem. You can't kill me, and if you fail, he'll kill you for failing." Maren set down her chopsticks. "Funny thing about surviving. It's mostly just refusing the obvious ending."

Vess looked at her own bowl. Steam rose from it, ordinary and warm. She realized she had not eaten a meal sitting down in six years.

"What's the obvious ending for me?" she asked.

"Your contract is done when I'm dead. Or when you are." Maren shrugged. "Those aren't the only two doors, though. People forget to look for the third one."

---

It took four months. Vess did what she did best, which was to find a man who thought himself untouchable and arrange a conversation with him. She did not kill Corvane. She took his ledgers, his keys, and his name, and she delivered them in a sealed case to the magistrates of Dessa Prime, with a note that said only: *He will not be sending anyone.*

Then she vanished, which was also something she did well.

---

Years later, in a harbor town on a world with a pale green sky, a woman running a small noodle stall noticed a stranger watching from across the street. The stranger raised two fingers.

Maren Tolliver, who had never once been finished, smiled and set out two bowls.
