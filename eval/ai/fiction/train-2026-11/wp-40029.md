# Something Borrowed

It started with my left hand.

I noticed it during the processional, when the string quartet swung into Pachelbel and every head in St. Agnes turned toward the doors. My fingers curled into a fist without my permission. I uncurled them. They curled again, slower this time, almost thoughtfully, like a cat testing a new room.

*Not now,* I thought. *Please, not now.*

It had been three weeks since I opened the ring box. My grandmother's ring, passed down from her grandmother, wrapped in yellowed silk that smelled of cedar and something older. Since then: the dreams of a stone house by the sea. The mornings I woke up standing at the window. The voice that wasn't my voice, humming hymns I'd never learned.

I told myself it was nerves. Everyone gets cold feet.

Then Clara appeared in the doorway, and for one perfect moment the thing inside me went quiet. She was luminous. Ivory silk, her mother's veil, a smile that trembled at the corners because she always cried at weddings, even her own. I loved her so much it felt like a bruise.

*Mine,* said the voice, and it wasn't talking about my love.

My knees locked. I swayed, and my best man, Theo, caught my elbow.

"You good?" he whispered.

I tried to say *no*. I tried to say *get her out of here*. What came out was a laugh, warm and easy, and my own mouth said, "Never better."

Theo grinned and let go.

Clara reached the altar. Her father placed her hand in mine, and I felt the cold move up my arm like water filling a pipe. My shoulder. My neck. The back of my jaw. I could still see, still hear, but the world had acquired a pane of glass between us, and I was on the wrong side of it.

"Dearly beloved," began Father Okonkwo.

I screamed. Nobody heard. The scream went somewhere inside me, down a long corridor, and echoed back.

*Who are you?* I demanded.

The answer came not in words but in images: a man in a black coat on a cliff. A woman in white, running. A church on fire. A ring, thrown into the sea and retrieved by a fisherman's net a hundred years later. Patience, enormous and cold, like an ocean waiting for a stone to sink.

*She promised me,* the spirit said. *Before God, she promised me, and then she ran. I have waited. I have waited in gold and silk and dark drawers. And now I will finish the vow.*

*Clara isn't her,* I begged. *She's not anyone you know. Please.*

*All brides are her.*

Father Okonkwo turned to me. "Do you, Daniel, take Clara to be your lawfully wedded wife?"

I threw everything I had at my throat. Every memory of us: the night we met at that terrible karaoke bar, the way she sang Fleetwood Mac completely off-key and didn't care. The apartment with the radiator that clanked. The night her mother died and I held her until sunrise. I built a wall of it, brick by brick, and pressed it against the cold.

For a second, my lips trembled. A syllable fought its way up. "N—"

The spirit crushed it like a moth.

"I do," I said. My voice was rich and steady. A stranger's voice wearing my face.

Clara's eyes were shining. Her turn. Father Okonkwo smiled. "And do you, Clara, take Daniel—"

"I do," she said, before he'd finished.

The guests laughed fondly. Eager bride.

But I was close enough to see it. Something moved behind her eyes. A flicker, like a candle guttering. Her pupils swelled, black swallowing hazel, and then settled.

She leaned toward me as if to share a secret, and her lips brushed my ear.

"Hello, Edmund," she whispered, in a voice like salt wind. "Did you really think I ran *away* from you? I ran to the sea witch. I bargained for the long road." Her fingers tightened on mine, cold as mine had become. "Three hundred years, I've waited too. In her mother's veil. Something borrowed."

The thing in my body went utterly still.

For the first time in three weeks, I felt it afraid.

"You may kiss the bride," said Father Okonkwo.

And deep inside her, down her own long corridor, behind her own pane of glass, I saw her. My Clara. Real Clara. Pressing her palms against the dark, looking straight at me.

She had felt it too. Her mother's veil. The dreams of fire.

Two prisoners, on an altar, in front of everyone we loved.

The spirits leaned in to kiss, and somewhere beneath them, Clara and I reached for each other.

And our hands, just barely, touched.
