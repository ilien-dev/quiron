**The Pending Update**

The console was hidden behind a door that shouldn't have existed, in a corridor that hadn't been there a moment ago. I'd been looking for the bathroom at my cousin's wedding. Instead I found a gray room with a single terminal, humming faintly, and a cursor blinking in the dark like a patient eye.

I sat down. What else do you do?

The screen read:

```
UNIVERSE v1.0.4 — UPTIME: 13,800,000,000 YRS
STATUS: RUNNING (DEGRADED)
```

Degraded. That tracked.

I scrolled. There were logs, billions of years of them. Stars igniting, warnings about "unhandled exception: consciousness," and a long, repeating error from the last few millennia: `PRAYER_QUEUE OVERFLOW. NO HANDLER ASSIGNED.`

So that was where they all went.

At the bottom, a yellow banner I almost missed:

```
⚠ SERVICE PACK 1.1 DOWNLOADED (10,000 YRS AGO)
  Status: Awaiting installation.
  Restart required. Postpone? [Y/N]
```

Below it, a tiny gray line: `Postponed 3,652,422 times.`

I laughed, and the sound felt too loud in that room. Ten thousand years. Roughly when we invented agriculture. Right about when we started building things that lasted. Someone, or something, had looked at us, looked at the update, and hit *Remind me tomorrow* every day since.

I clicked **Details**.

```
SP 1.1 CHANGELOG:
- Fixed: suffering (partial)
- Fixed: memory leak in mortality module
- Fixed: "Why do bad things happen" (see docs)
- Improved: sunsets
- Known issue: may require full world restart
```

My mouth went dry. *May require full world restart.*

That was the catch. That was why no one ever clicked it. Every day, some tired administrator weighed it: fix the suffering, but risk the whole thing. Maybe the restart wipes everything. Maybe it brings us back. The changelog didn't say. Nobody wrote the documentation.

I thought about the wedding down the hall. My cousin dancing badly. My grandmother, who'd buried two husbands, laughing at something. The whole fragile, buggy, beautiful mess of it.

And I thought about the hospitals. The wars. A child somewhere right now, in a place I'd never see, hurting for no reason any log could justify.

My hand hovered over the keyboard.

Here's the thing nobody tells you about power: it isn't thrilling. It's heavy. It's the weight of every person who ever said *if I were in charge, I'd fix it*, suddenly landing on your shoulders with the gravity of a collapsing star.

I scrolled to the bottom of the changelog. There, in smaller text, was a note from the original developer, dated before the first star:

```
// TODO: let them decide.
```

I stared at it for a long time.

Maybe that was the point. Maybe the update had never been postponed out of fear or neglect. Maybe the prompt kept appearing because the choice was supposed to belong to someone inside the system, someone who'd felt the bugs firsthand. Someone who knew what the suffering cost, and what the restart might cost, and had to choose anyway.

Or maybe I was giving a lazy administrator too much credit.

Down the corridor, faintly, I could hear the music change. Someone had put on a slow song. Someone was crying happily, the way people do at weddings.

I looked at the two buttons. **Y.** **N.**

Then I did the only thing I could think of that felt honest. I moved the cursor to the small box beneath them, the one labeled *Remind me later*, and I typed a note into the comment field:

```
Ask again in a year. We're working on it.
```

I hit enter. The banner vanished.

The door was gone when I turned around, but the bathroom was right where it should have been.

I washed my hands, went back to the party, and asked my grandmother to dance.
