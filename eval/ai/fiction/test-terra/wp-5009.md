The elevator doors opened onto the roof, and Mara stepped into a storm of blue light.

Below her, the city shone like a circuit board. Above her, the machine hung between four steel towers, its rings turning around a small, brilliant sun.

At the center of the roof stood Dr. Voss, wearing the white coat he’d worn in every broadcast. Six guards stood behind him. A seventh was by the elevator, eating a packet of cheese crackers.

“You’re late,” Voss said.

Mara drew her pistol. “Turn it off.”

The guard with the crackers folded the packet shut. He was a narrow man with a mustache that seemed to have been applied in a hurry.

Voss smiled. “Do you know what your father said to me, the night he died?”

Mara’s hand tightened around the pistol.

“He said—”

The guard shot her.

The sound cracked across the roof. Mara fell against the elevator doors and slid down them, leaving a dark smear on the polished steel.

For a moment, nobody moved.

Voss looked at the guard. “What have you done?”

The guard lowered his gun. “She had a weapon, sir.”

“I could see that.”

“She was pointing it at you.”

“Yes, Martin. I was speaking to her.”

Martin glanced at Mara. “Sorry.”

Voss stared at him, then crossed the roof and knelt beside her. He pressed two fingers to her neck. When he stood again, rain had begun to spot his white coat.

“She was the only person who knew the shutdown sequence,” he said.

Martin considered this. “Could we look it up?”

“I erased it.”

“Why?”

“So she would have to come here.”

The other guards watched the rings turn. One of them quietly put away a sandwich.

Voss returned to his console and began striking keys. The machine responded with a low, rising hum.

“Did you tell her what her father said?” Martin asked.

“No.”

“What was it?”

Voss’s fingers stopped. “He said, ‘Please don’t kill me.’”

“Oh.”

“I had prepared a different version.”

The sun inside the machine pulsed. Across the city, every window went black.

Voss swallowed and typed faster. The console flashed red.

**BIOMETRIC AUTHORIZATION REQUIRED.**

He looked at Mara.

The guards looked at Mara.

“Oh,” Martin said again.

They carried her to the console. Voss lifted her hand and pressed it to the scanner. Nothing happened.

“Perhaps the other hand,” Martin suggested.

“It scans living tissue.”

They tried the other hand anyway.

The hum deepened into a vibration that passed through their boots. Far below, car horns began to sound.

Voss opened a panel beneath the console. Inside was a dense knot of wires, each one neatly labeled in his own handwriting.

Martin leaned over his shoulder. “Can you cut the power?”

“The machine generates its own power.”

“Can you cut whatever it’s generating power for?”

Voss followed a thick cable with his eyes. It ran from the console to the nearest tower, where a small junction box had been bolted to the frame.

“That controls the magnetic containment field,” he said.

“And if we cut it?”

“The core will collapse.”

Martin waited.

“Possibly.”

“Possibly immediately?”

“Possibly the core will collapse. It may instead expand.”

“How much?”

Voss looked up at the little sun.

A loose cracker slipped from Martin’s pocket and skittered across the roof. He watched it go.

“Any other ideas?” he asked.

Voss closed the panel. He gave Martin a pair of insulated cutters and showed him which cable to cut. Then he sent the other guards down the elevator.

The two men stood at the junction box in the rain.

“I really am sorry,” Martin said.

Voss nodded. “Cut it.”

Martin squeezed the handles.

The cable sparked. For one terrible second, the sun swelled, flooding the roof with white.

Then it went out.

The rings slowed. Somewhere below, the lights came back on, block by block, until the whole city was visible again.

Voss sat down heavily. He stared at Mara’s body by the console, and Martin went to fetch his coat to cover her.

When the elevator returned, it brought three of the guards and a paramedic. Voss got up to meet them. Martin handed over his gun without being asked.
