# Notes From the Other Side

Day one is mostly a blur. I remember the bite, which was on my forearm and honestly not as bad as I'd expected. I remember the fever. I remember lying on the floor of the laundromat thinking, very clearly, *well, this is embarrassing*.

And then I remember standing up.

---

Here's the first thing nobody tells you about being a zombie: you're still in there.

Not all the way. It's like being in the back seat of a car someone else is driving. You can see out the windshield. You can have opinions about the route. You just can't reach the wheel.

The body wants one thing, and it wants it all the time, and it's not subtle about it. The hunger isn't in your stomach. It's everywhere. It's in your teeth and your fingers and the backs of your eyes. It feels like being very, very thirsty, if thirst had a direction and a smell.

I'm not going to talk about what it's for. You know what it's for.

---

The second thing nobody tells you is how boring it is.

The movies make it look like it's all chasing and screaming. It's not. Most of the time, we just stand around. We drift. There's a group of maybe forty of us who hang out in the parking lot of the Kroger on Elm, shuffling in slow circles like we're waiting for a store that's never going to open.

I've gotten to know some of them. Not *know* know. We can't talk. But you start to recognize people.

There's a guy in a Kroger apron who I'm pretty sure worked here. He still tries to line up the shopping carts. He can't do it, his hands don't work right, but he keeps pushing them toward the corral like it matters.

There's a woman in running clothes who's always facing east. Every morning, when the sun comes up, she turns toward it. I don't know why. Maybe she used to run at dawn.

There's a kid. I try not to look at the kid.

---

I used to be an accountant. I want that on the record. My name is Daniel Okafor, and I was thirty-four, and I did people's taxes, and I was good at it. I had a cat named Deductible. I hope somebody let her out.

I think about her a lot, in here. I think about all sorts of things. My mom's jollof rice. The apartment I was going to put a deposit on. The fact that I never finished *The Brothers Karamazov*, which, honestly, I probably wasn't going to finish anyway.

The body doesn't care about any of that. The body lurches after pigeons.

---

Sometimes there are survivors.

We hear them before we see them. A car engine. A dropped can. And all forty of us turn at once, like a school of fish, and start walking.

I hate this part. I hate it more than anything.

Because I can't stop. I scream at my legs to stop, and they don't. I scream at my hands, and they reach anyway. Last week there was a man with a shotgun on top of a pickup truck, and he was shouting at us, and I was right up front, and I remember thinking, *please, please aim for me*.

He didn't. He got in the truck and drove away, and I was so relieved I would have cried if I still could.

---

I've been noticing something. I don't know if it's real.

Some days, the driver's grip seems a little looser. Last Tuesday, a pigeon landed right in front of me, and the body lunged for it, and I thought *no*, really hard, and my hand stopped.

It was only for a second. Maybe less. Then the hunger swallowed it back up.

But it stopped.

I haven't told anyone, obviously. I can't. But I watch the others now, looking for the same thing. And I think maybe the woman in the running clothes isn't just facing the sunrise out of habit. I think maybe she's choosing to.

---

It's night again. The parking lot is quiet. The Kroger guy is pushing a cart in slow circles. The streetlights came back on a few days ago, which means someone out there is running a generator, which means someone's rebuilding.

I think about that a lot.

I think about walking up to them one day, slowly, with my hands down, and trying really hard to make my mouth say *Daniel*.

I don't know if I'll ever manage it. I don't know if they'd wait long enough to hear it.

But I'm practicing. Every night, when the lot is quiet, I pick one finger, and I try to hold it still.

Tonight I got to a count of three.
