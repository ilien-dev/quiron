Sir Aldren had prepared for fire.

His shield was layered with salamander hide. His cloak had been soaked in witchwater. His eyebrows had been shaved off in advance, which the royal alchemist assured him would save time later.

For three weeks he rode north, following a trail of scorched villages, terrified shepherds, and increasingly dramatic warning signs.

TURN BACK, read the first.

CERTAIN DEATH, promised the second.

DRAGON AHEAD, declared the third, beneath which someone had added:

ONE NIGHT ONLY.

At sunset, Aldren reached the tower. It stood alone in a blackened field, crooked as an accusing finger. A single window glowed near the top.

Aldren dismounted and drew his sword.

“Dragon!” he shouted. “Come forth!”

Thunder answered.

Then drums.

A man stepped from behind the tower wearing a crimson coat, twelve gold chains, and boots apparently made from mirrors. He was followed by two lute players and a boy carrying a smoking brass bowl.

“Who calls out Dragon?” the man demanded.

Aldren raised his shield. “I am Sir Aldren of Westmere, sworn sword of King Edric. Release Princess Lenora or taste steel!”

The man stared at him.

Then he turned to the lute players. “Drop it.”

They struck a vicious rhythm.

Dragon spread his arms.

“Sir Aldren rides in with his sword held high,  
No brows on his face and fear in his eye—”

“My eye contains no fear!”

“Interrupt again and your horse gets a verse.”

Aldren’s mare quietly backed away.

Dragon paced around the knight, words pouring from him faster than arrows.

“You came for a monster, you came for flame,  
But you walked in blind when you heard my name.  
The king sent a pawn with a shield and a brand—  
You’re saving a princess who already had plans.”

The music stopped.

Aldren lowered his sword slightly. “What plans?”

A rope ladder tumbled from the tower window. Princess Lenora climbed down wearing trousers, a leather vest, and an expression of deep annoyance.

“You took long enough,” she said.

“Your Highness!” Aldren rushed forward. “Stand behind me. This Dragon has bewitched you.”

“No, Father imprisoned me.”

Aldren stopped.

Lenora pointed at the tower. “I refused to marry Duke Ormund, so Father locked me up and announced I’d been abducted. He expected Ormund to ‘rescue’ me. Dragon happened to be passing through.”

“Touring,” Dragon said. “My kingdomwide tour. The Blackened Field show sold out.”

“The scorched villages?”

“Stage effects.”

The boy proudly lifted his smoking bowl.

“And the terrified shepherds?”

Dragon shrugged. “Critics.”

Aldren struggled to fit this new information inside his helmet. “But the king commanded me—”

“To bring me home,” Lenora said. “Where I’ll be married before breakfast.”

“You are the princess.”

“Yes.”

“It is your duty.”

Dragon winced. “Terrible opening. Weak rhythm. No heart.”

“No one asked you,” Aldren snapped.

The lute players gasped.

Dragon stepped closer. “Then answer her, knight. Not the crown. Not the gold. Her.”

Aldren looked at Lenora. She did not seem bewitched. She seemed tired, angry, and very certain.

“What do you want?” he asked.

Her expression softened.

“I want to reach the Free Cities. I can study law there. Dragon offered me passage with his troupe.”

“My carriage has velvet seats,” Dragon added.

Aldren glanced back at the southern road. Returning without Lenora meant disgrace. Perhaps prison. Definitely a lecture from the king, who could make the word *disappointed* last half an hour.

Returning with her meant delivering a prisoner.

He sheathed his sword.

“The dragon attacked me,” he said slowly. “There was a tremendous battle. I was overcome.”

Dragon grinned. “Go on.”

“The princess was carried beyond pursuit.”

“Better.”

“I barely escaped with my life.”

“Now give it thunder.”

Aldren climbed onto his mare, then paused. “They will send others.”

Lenora tossed him a purse of gold. “Then you’d better make the monster convincing.”

The next morning, Sir Aldren rode into the capital with cracked armor, a burned cloak, and one eyebrow painted on crookedly. Before thousands in the royal square, he described a beast forty feet tall, with golden scales, jeweled teeth, and a roar that shook the moon.

Far north, Dragon’s troupe rolled toward freedom while Lenora practiced legal arguments between performances.

By winter, every knight in the kingdom feared the terrible creature beyond the Blackened Field.

By spring, they were repeating his verses.

And by summer, Dragon was one night only everywhere.
