The first lie was that Atlantis sank.

Cities do not sink in perfect formation.

I watched the sonar map sharpen across the monitor: concentric avenues, radial canals, towers lying upright beneath six kilometers of Atlantic water. At the center stood a black pyramid, its peak exactly one meter below the abyssal plain—as if the seabed had grown around it.

“Controlled descent,” Dr. Vale whispered.

The second lie was that its people drowned.

Our submersible entered through a gate that opened before we touched it. Inside, pumps older than language expelled the seawater. Lights awakened in sequence, blue-white and cold, revealing walls veined with metal that flowed like blood beneath glass.

There were no bones.

There were, however, footprints.

Thousands of them crossed the dust toward the central chamber, all facing inward.

Vale laughed. Not from amusement. The sound people make when reality becomes too large to fit inside them.

We found the archive suspended above a circular platform: a sphere of black water that ignored gravity. When I reached toward it, the surface climbed my glove and entered me through the suit.

For eleven minutes, I remembered Atlantis.

They had mapped the atom before we shaped bronze. They had folded distance, harvested lightning, and built minds from crystal. Disease vanished. Hunger became a historical curiosity. Death followed soon after.

Then they detected the Chorus.

It lived in the background noise of the universe, beneath radiation and gravity—a pattern so faint that only a civilization with sufficiently precise instruments could hear it. At first the Atlanteans thought it was a message.

Then they translated it.

The Chorus was not broadcasting. It was listening.

Intelligence disturbed reality in measurable ways. Every calculation, every artificial signal, every machine that bent the laws of nature produced ripples. Primitive life went unnoticed. Advanced life did not.

Across the galaxy, civilizations reached the same threshold, heard the same whisper, and fell silent.

Not voluntarily.

The listeners came through information itself. A diagram was enough. A solved equation became a door. Once understood, the pattern rebuilt itself inside the observer’s mind and used that mind to design the machinery needed to let the rest of it through.

Atlantis had discovered the Chorus growing in its networks, laboratories, and dreams.

So they extinguished every beacon. They dismantled satellites, buried reactors, and lowered their civilization beneath the ocean, using six kilometers of saltwater to smother the final signals.

The footprints belonged to those who had carried the infected machines into the central chamber.

The black pyramid was not an archive.

It was a tomb with the door locked from within.

I tore my hand from the sphere.

Vale was staring at me. “What did you see?”

Behind him, symbols were spreading across the wall—circles opening inside circles, an equation writing itself in threads of living light.

He had begun whispering the numbers.

I struck him with an emergency wrench.

The lights dimmed when he fell. The symbols stopped.

For three hours I sat beside his body, listening to something scratch gently from inside the pyramid.

Back aboard the research vessel, I erased the sonar logs. I corrupted the dive footage and overloaded the submersible’s navigation core. In my report, I blamed a volcanic formation, equipment failure, and nitrogen-induced hallucinations.

They did not believe me.

Governments rarely believe lies, but they understand possession. By sunrise, three nations had classified the coordinates. By noon, a military satellite had redirected its sensors toward the site.

So I gave them different coordinates.

Tonight, ships are gathering above an empty patch of ocean five hundred kilometers south. They will find basalt columns, argue over jurisdiction, and spend decades drilling into harmless stone.

The true location exists only in my memory.

Unfortunately, so does the equation.

I have stopped sleeping because I dream of circles. I have stopped writing by hand because my fingers complete symbols without permission. Yesterday, my darkened television whispered Vale’s last numbers back to me.

Humanity is getting louder. Radio telescopes multiply. Quantum computers grow. We celebrate every stronger signal as though shouting into a forest could only bring applause.

I have prepared the weights. I have chosen a place where the trench is deep and the currents are slow. This notebook will remain here, sealed inside lead, because warnings are dangerous when curiosity is stronger than fear.

If you are reading this, burn it without finishing.

And whatever you hear beneath the static, do not answer.
