# Temporal Makeover Inc.

The waiting room at TMI smelled like burnt sugar and ozone, which the receptionist explained was just what happens when you compress seven days of causality into a lobby that small. Marisol didn't ask for clarification. She'd learned in the last hour that clarification from TMI staff tended to raise more questions than it answered.

"Ms. Alvarez?" A man in a gray suit that seemed to shimmer slightly at the edges, like a heat mirage wearing clothes, gestured her toward a door marked CONSULTATION 4. "I'm Desmond, I'll be handling your revision today. I understand you want to revisit the incident from last Thursday."

"I want to un-fire someone," Marisol said, sitting down across from a desk that had no visible drawers and a single glowing tablet on its surface. "My assistant manager. I fired her in front of the whole team because I was having a bad week, and it was cruel, and I can't stop thinking about it."

Desmond nodded, tapping through something on the tablet. "That's a Category Two revision — emotional decision, low-stakes execution, no third-party fatalities or major financial shifts. Very doable. Very standard, honestly. Most of our Thursday clients are undoing arguments with their mothers." He glanced up. "The fee is four thousand, and the compliance terms are non-negotiable. You understand the seven-day window rule?"

"Only things from the last seven days. I looked it up."

"And the causality dampening clause. We can change your action, but we can't guarantee the world adjusts smoothly around it. There may be ripples. Minor ones, in your case — we've modeled it, and the assistant manager's schedule that week barely changes." He slid a contract across the desk. "Sign here, here, and initial the ripple waiver."

She signed. The pen felt colder than a pen should.

"One more thing," Desmond said, almost as an afterthought, tapping a final box on the tablet. "Standard question, required by regulation. Are you aware of any other TMI clients who may have modified actions or decisions affecting the same seven-day window?"

"No," Marisol said. "Why would I be?"

Desmond's smile flickered, just slightly, in a way she'd remember later and wish she'd asked about. "No reason. Just paperwork."

The revision itself felt like nothing — a blink, a held breath, and then she was standing in her office again, and the memory of firing Priya had rearranged itself in her head into a memory of not firing her, of biting her tongue instead and taking a walk around the block to cool off. Clean. Simple. Exactly as promised.

Except Priya wasn't at her desk on Monday.

"She quit," said Devon from accounting, not looking up from his screen. "Friday afternoon. No notice, just cleaned out her desk and left. Didn't say why."

Marisol felt something cold drop through her stomach. In the version of the week she now remembered, she hadn't fired Priya. She'd been kind. There was no reason for Priya to quit in that version, no scene, no humiliation, nothing to run from.

She called TMI from the parking garage, hands shaking.

"I need to speak to Desmond. Consultation Four. Something's gone wrong with my revision."

The hold music was a looped, tinny version of a song she was almost sure hadn't been written yet. When Desmond finally answered, his voice had lost its polish.

"Ms. Alvarez. I was about to call you, actually. We've had a — complication."

"Priya quit. In the new timeline. She wasn't even fired and she still left."

A pause, longer than she liked. "It appears," Desmond said carefully, "that another client filed a Category Two revision touching the same seven-day window, roughly four hours after yours processed. Regulation requires clients to disclose overlapping edits, but this particular client did not disclose, which is — as you might imagine — a serious compliance violation on our end as well as theirs."

"Who was it? What did they change?"

"I'm not permitted to disclose client information. But I can tell you the revision involved a promotion offer. One that, in the original timeline, your assistant manager received the same week you fired her — and accepted, out of relief, out of vindication, out of needing to feel valued somewhere. In your new, kinder timeline, she never had a reason to feel that way. The offer came anyway. And this time, with nothing to hold her here, she took it and left without a second thought."

Marisol sat in her car for a long moment, watching the parking garage's fluorescent lights flicker.

"Undo it," she said. "Both of it. Put it back exactly the way it was."

"I'm afraid," Desmond said, and for the first time all day he sounded almost sorry, "that's not how the seven-day window works once it's closed. You get one clean edit per client, Ms. Alvarez. That's the rule everyone signs. It was cruel of you to fire her. It was also, as it turns out, the only version of that week where she stayed."

The line went dead, and Marisol sat there a long time, thinking about how some mistakes, it seemed, weren't hers to fix after all.
