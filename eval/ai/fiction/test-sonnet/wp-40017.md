# Continuity of Government

The Secret Service calls it Continuity of Government, and nobody in the room tonight is thinking about how funny that phrase is about to become.

I have been the president's chief of staff for four years, long enough to know the sound of his footsteps in the hallway, the rhythm of his breathing when he's nervous versus when he's merely tired, the particular way he clears his throat before he says something he doesn't quite believe himself. I know him the way you know a spouse. Which is why, at 11:47 PM on a Tuesday in March, standing in the doorway of the Oval Office with a cup of coffee going cold in my hand, I know immediately that the man behind the desk is not the president.

It isn't anything dramatic. He's wearing the right suit. He has the right slightly-too-big ears, the right silver at his temples. But he's holding his pen in his left hand, and the president is right-handed, has been right-handed for the sixty-one years I've had access to his medical file, and no stroke, no injury, no crisis of the last six hours would explain a man switching hands to sign a document that doesn't require a signature at all.

I don't say anything. I set the coffee down, very calmly, and I walk out of the room, and I call a number that only four people in this administration know exists.

Here is the thing nobody prepared me for: shapeshifters, once you know to look, are everywhere.

The response team that arrives isn't Secret Service. They're something else, something older, wearing suits that fit a little too perfectly and carrying devices I don't recognize, and the woman in charge introduces herself as Agent Okafor and asks me, without any preamble, whether the thing in the Oval Office moved like it was thinking through each gesture.

"Yes," I say. "Like he was performing being him instead of just being him."

She nods like I've confirmed something she already suspected. "That's the tell. They're good, but the good ones give themselves away by trying too hard to be natural. It's the newer ones — younger, less practiced — that overcompensate."

"The newer ones. As opposed to—"

"As opposed to the one already living in Housing and Urban Development. Five years now. Very settled in. Doesn't blink enough, if you know what you're counting."

I feel my stomach drop somewhere around my shoes.

"You're telling me there's more than one."

Agent Okafor gives me a look that manages to be both sympathetic and deeply unsurprised by my naivety. "Mr. Whitfield, we have been managing an infiltration problem in this government for longer than either of us has been alive. It ebbs and flows. Sometimes it's a junior staffer nobody notices for a decade. Sometimes—" she nods toward the Oval Office, where the muffled sound of someone signing documents in the wrong hand continues, oblivious "—it's more ambitious than that."

"How many. Total. Right now, in this administration."

"That's need-to-know, and you don't need to."

"I think I do, actually, given that I just watched one try to become my boss."

She almost smiles. "Fair. Three, that we're certain of. Possibly a fourth we're tracking in Commerce, but the signature's inconsistent, which usually just means someone with a strange gait and a cold, not our friends."

"And the real president—"

"—is currently unconscious in a subbasement three blocks from here, which is where our people are headed right now, and which is also where you're about to be escorted, because you know too much to be left wandering a building with an active infiltration in it."

I want to ask what happens to the thing sitting in the Oval Office right now, the thing wearing my friend's face and signing documents with the wrong hand, but Agent Okafor is already moving, already talking into a wrist mic in clipped code I don't understand, and two of her people are steering me firmly by the elbows toward a stairwell that supposedly doesn't exist on any floor plan I've ever seen.

Behind us, through the closing doors, I hear the false president laugh at something — a low, careful laugh, rehearsed, almost right — and I understand, with a cold clarity that will not leave me for the rest of my life, that the country has been quietly governed by something else for longer than anyone in power has been willing to admit, and that tonight is just the first time in years someone happened to be paying close enough attention to notice.
