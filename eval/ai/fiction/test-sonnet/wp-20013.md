# First in Line

The fire doesn't hurt, which is the first thing I notice, and somehow that's worse than if it did.

I'm standing on a plain of black glass that stretches out in every direction until it curls up into a horizon made of flame. There's no sun, just a sourceless orange light that pulses like something breathing. The air smells like a struck match, and it's hot enough that sweat should be pouring off me, but I'm not sweating. I'm not doing much of anything except standing here, turning in a slow circle, looking for another person and finding absolutely no one.

"Hello?" My voice doesn't echo. It just gets swallowed.

I walk for a while. There's no way to tell how long — no sun to track, no watch on my wrist, though I don't remember losing a watch, or putting one on in the first place. Geysers of flame erupt out of cracks in the glass every so often, close enough that I flinch, but they never touch me. It's like the fire knows where I am and has decided, for now, to leave me alone.

Eventually I stop walking because there's nowhere in particular to walk to, and I sit down on the glass, which is somehow cool against the backs of my legs despite everything else radiating heat, and I wait.

That's when he shows up.

He doesn't appear in a burst of smoke or a crack of thunder. He just walks out of the shimmer of heat on the horizon like a man walking out of fog, dressed in a plain gray suit that seems wrong for the setting, like he raided a mid-tier department store on his way in. He's not what I expected — no horns, no tail, no pitchfork, just an ordinary-looking man with tired eyes and the kind of posture that suggests he's been standing for centuries and his back has opinions about it.

He stops a few feet from me and looks almost surprised.

"Well," he says. "Finally."

"Finally?"

"You're the first to arrive." He says it the way someone might announce a delayed flight has landed. Mild relief, mild annoyance that it took so long. "Do you have any idea how long I've been standing around down here with nothing to do? Centuries. Well — not centuries here, obviously, time doesn't move the same way, but it felt like centuries. Felt like it, and feeling is the only currency that matters in a place like this, so it counts."

I don't know what to say to that, so I say nothing.

He crouches down until we're roughly eye level, resting his elbows on his knees, studying me the way you'd study a crossword clue you're pretty sure you know but want to double check.

"So," he says. "Tell me. Who are you?"

"I—" I open my mouth and realize I don't actually know. Not for certain. There's a name sitting somewhere behind my teeth, but when I reach for it, it slides away like a fish in murky water. "I don't remember."

"That's alright. Most people don't, not right away. It comes back." He waves a hand, unconcerned. "What did you do, then? To end up here, I mean. Everyone ends up here for a reason. I'd like to know mine, for once — what the reason was for the very first."

I search myself for guilt, for the shape of a sin large enough to earn eternal fire, and I find nothing but a strange, quiet blankness, like a room that's been cleared out before you arrived to inspect it.

"I don't know that either."

Something flickers across his face — not anger, more like disappointment, the kind a teacher gets when the star student blanks on the first question of the year.

"And how did you die?"

I try that one too. I reach for the last thing I remember — a hospital, maybe, or a car, or nothing at all, just a soft closing-down, like a screen going dark — and it's gone before I can hold it, the same way the rest is gone.

"I don't remember that either," I say. "I'm sorry."

He's quiet for a long moment, looking at me with something that isn't quite pity. Somewhere behind him, a geyser of flame roars up out of the glass and dies away again.

"Well," he says finally, straightening up, brushing invisible dust from knees that have never touched dust. "I suppose we have time to figure it out. We have, after all, absolutely nothing else."

He sits down across from me on the black glass, and behind him, the fire keeps burning, forever, for no one else to see.
