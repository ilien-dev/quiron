# strategic-communication

**A best-of-breed language with a holistic approach to moving the needle.**

Welcome to strategic-communication, a language for expressing ambitious ideas with confidence, alignment, and a healthy respect for the phrase “going forward.”

Whether you’re socializing a vision, unpacking a challenge, or circling back on a conversation that could have been an email, strategic-communication gives your message the executive presence it deserves.

## The vision

Modern work moves fast. Priorities shift, stakeholders multiply, and every initiative has at least three workstreams. In this environment, ordinary language can struggle to keep pace.

Strategic-communication meets that moment by bringing familiar concepts into a more synergistic orbit. It helps teams reframe problems as opportunities, decisions as journeys, and meetings as valuable touchpoints.

Our north star is simple: create space for conversations that sound like progress.

## Core principles

**Alignment before action.** Make sure everyone is moving in the same direction before anyone asks where you’re going.

**Clarity at scale.** A message should be broad enough to include every stakeholder and specific enough to fit on a slide.

**Continuous optimization.** If a sentence works, consider whether it could work harder.

**Outcome-oriented thinking.** Focus on moving the needle, even when the needle has yet to be baselined.

**A holistic perspective.** Look at the big picture, the granular details, and the space between them. Ideally, in the same sentence.

## Getting started

No installation is required to begin thinking strategically. Start with a straightforward statement:

> We need to decide what to do next.

Identify its core components: a need, a decision, and a next step. Then elevate the framing:

> Let’s align on the path forward and operationalize our next phase.

For a more collaborative tone, invite stakeholders into the process:

> Let’s create a touchpoint to align key stakeholders on the path forward and operationalize our next phase.

You’ve now transformed a simple request into a platform for cross-functional engagement.

## A practical vocabulary

Strategic-communication is built around a flexible set of expressions. Combine them to suit your audience, objective, and available meeting time.

| When you mean… | Consider saying… |
| --- | --- |
| Talk about it | Socialize the idea |
| Discuss it later | Circle back |
| Start working | Operationalize the strategy |
| Make progress | Move the needle |
| Agree on a plan | Align on the path forward |
| Understand the details | Double-click on the problem |
| Use what we have | Leverage existing assets |
| Decide who does what | Clarify ownership across workstreams |
| Something went wrong | We have an opportunity to learn |
| We don’t know yet | We’re still pressure-testing the approach |

Context matters. “Circle back” can mean anything from “I’ll reply this afternoon” to “This item will live in a document indefinitely.” Add a date when the outcome depends on knowing which one you mean.

## Usage patterns

### Frame an initiative

Begin with a broad aspiration, connect it to an organizational priority, and close with an outcome:

> We’re taking a holistic approach to the customer journey, aligning cross-functional capabilities to unlock sustainable impact.

### Surface a concern

Keep the conversation constructive by turning obstacles into opportunities:

> We have an opportunity to pressure-test our assumptions and ensure the operating model is fit for purpose.

### Request a decision

Bring the discussion toward a concrete next step:

> To maintain momentum, can we align on a decision owner and confirm the path forward by Friday?

The final example includes an owner and a date. Strategic language works best when it gives people enough information to act.

## Guiding questions

Before sharing a message, take a moment to pressure-test it:

1. **Audience:** Who needs to understand or act on this?
2. **Outcome:** What should happen next?
3. **Ownership:** Who is responsible for making it happen?
4. **Timing:** When is a response or decision needed?
5. **Signal:** Are the important details visible beneath the strategic framing?

If the answers are unclear, simplify. A well-placed “Please send the draft by Tuesday” can do more for alignment than an entire page of thought leadership.

## Contributing

Contributions are welcome from thought leaders, subject matter experts, and anyone who has ever been asked to “take this offline.”

To propose an addition, bring a phrase, explain what it means in plain language, and include an example of when it might be useful. Especially valuable contributions help readers communicate with both confidence and specificity.

Please keep suggestions inclusive, constructive, and easy to understand. We’re building a shared vocabulary, so everyone should be able to participate in the conversation.

## The path forward

Strategic-communication is a reminder that language shapes how teams work together. A memorable phrase can create momentum, but a clear request, an accountable owner, and a meaningful deadline are what help a team deliver.

Let’s align, get to work, and move that needle.
