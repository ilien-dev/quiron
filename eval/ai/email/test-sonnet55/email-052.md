Houston, Nov. 13: Enron Corp. (ENE) received a $1.5 billion cash infusion from Dynegy Inc. (DYN) on Tuesday. The money is meant to steady the energy trader as it works through a crisis of investor confidence and a tightening liquidity squeeze.

**Deal structure**

The investment is tied to the merger agreement announced last week, under which Dynegy would acquire Enron in a stock transaction. Dynegy is putting in the cash through a preferred equity investment in Northern Natural Gas, one of Enron's core pipeline assets. Dynegy would receive the pipeline as security if the broader merger does not close. People familiar with the arrangement said $500 million was due at signing and the remaining $1 billion is to follow at closing.

**Why it matters**

Enron has faced mounting pressure since mid-October, when it disclosed a large charge tied to off-balance-sheet partnerships and then restated several years of earnings. Its shares have lost most of their value this year. Trading partners have grown wary of extending credit, and rating agencies have signaled that further downgrades are possible. A cut to below investment grade could trigger accelerated debt payments and collateral demands that Enron would struggle to meet.

The infusion gives Enron immediate breathing room. Analysts said it should help the company meet near-term obligations and reassure counterparties that it can keep funding its trading book, which depends on market confidence.

**Dynegy's position**

For Dynegy, the transaction would be a transformative move, combining its generation and midstream operations with Enron's large wholesale trading franchise and pipeline network. Management has said the combination would create a leading player in North American energy markets. Dynegy's largest shareholder, ChevronTexaco, has backed the transaction and supported the financing.

Some investors are cautious. Questions remain about the full extent of Enron's liabilities, the quality of its earnings and the possibility of additional restatements. Analysts said Dynegy's willingness to proceed will depend on what its due diligence uncovers, and the deal contains conditions that could let it walk away if Enron's financial position deteriorates materially.

**Market reaction**

Enron shares were volatile in Tuesday's session, as traders weighed the relief from the cash against doubts about whether the merger will be completed. Dynegy shares also moved lower on concerns about the risks it is taking on. Enron's bonds remained under pressure, and credit default protection stayed expensive.

**What to watch**

- Any action by Moody's, S&P or Fitch on Enron's credit rating
- Regulatory filings that may reveal further detail on Enron's obligations and off-balance-sheet entities
- Progress on the SEC's inquiry and any related shareholder litigation
- Antitrust and regulatory reviews of the proposed combination
- Statements from Dynegy management on due diligence and the timing of closing

The transaction is expected to need approval from both companies' shareholders and from regulators, with closing targeted for late next year.
