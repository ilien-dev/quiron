Enron’s collapse remains a striking example of how decisions made by a small group of powerful people can devastate thousands of lives. Once celebrated as an innovative energy company, Enron built its reputation on financial reports that concealed debt, inflated profits, and misled investors.

When the truth emerged in 2001, employees lost jobs and retirement savings, shareholders lost billions, and public confidence in corporate leadership suffered a serious blow. Many of those hurt had trusted executives who publicly promoted the company while privately protecting their own interests.

The scandal was bigger than a handful of dishonest executives. It exposed failures among auditors, board members, and financial institutions whose responsibility was to question suspicious practices and demand accountability.

Enron’s story reminds us that business ethics cannot be reduced to a mission statement. Transparency, independent oversight, and protection for employees who raise concerns are essential safeguards. Without them, ambition and greed can flourish unchecked, leaving ordinary people to bear the consequences.
