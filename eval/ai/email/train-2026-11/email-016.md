Utilities should also offer targeted incentives for commercial and industrial customers that temporarily reduce consumption. Assistance should help vulnerable households participate without compromising health or safety.

Where technically feasible, existing generation and storage resources should be made available during critical periods, subject to environmental requirements and reliability standards. Shared forecasts, consistent public messaging, and clearly assigned responsibilities would support faster decisions and reduce confusion during supply emergencies. Priority facilities, including hospitals, emergency shelters, and water treatment plants, should receive particular attention in contingency planning.

Implementation should begin with an assessment of expected shortages, available resources, and estimated costs. Progress should be measured through peak-demand reductions, emergency supply availability, customer participation, and service reliability. Regular updates should explain results, spending, and any adjustments needed.

This approach would provide immediate relief while preserving flexibility for investments in efficiency, transmission, storage, and cleaner generation. Its success depends on timely coordination, transparent oversight, and fair treatment of customers.
