# Top 5 Video Game WTFs!!

**[COLD OPEN]**

*(Upbeat music. Host stands in front of a gaming setup.)*

**HOST:** Video games are supposed to make sense, right? Rules, physics, a plot. Then you get these. Let's go!

**[INTRO]**

**HOST:** Welcome back, gamers! Here are the top five video game WTF moments.

**[NUMBER 5]**

**HOST:** Number five: MissingNo. In Pokémon Red and Blue, if you followed a specific glitch sequence, you could find a weird, garbled Pokémon with a glitched name. Some players got item duplication and wild data, and it's still a legend.

**[NUMBER 4]**

**HOST:** Number four: the Pac-Man kill screen. Make it to level 256 and the game breaks. Half the screen fills with garbled code and random symbols. The game's counter overflowed, and nobody could finish the level.

**[NUMBER 3]**

**HOST:** Number three: Goat Simulator. You play a goat. You headbutt things. You cause chaos. Its physics glitches are so ridiculous that they became part of the charm.

**[NUMBER 2]**

**HOST:** Number two: Superman 64. You fly through rings in a foggy city, and the game has a timer that's hard to beat. Let's just say it's one of the most infamous games ever released.

**[NUMBER 1]**

**HOST:** And number one: Duke Nukem Forever. It was in development for about fourteen years. When it finally came out in 2011, it was a mess of outdated jokes and gameplay. Fourteen years!
