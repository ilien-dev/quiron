# How An Infinite Hotel Ran Out Of Room

**[COLD OPEN]**

Imagine a hotel with infinitely many rooms. Every room is numbered: 1, 2, 3, and on forever. Now imagine it's completely full, and a new guest walks up to the front desk and asks for a room.

Most people would say the answer is no. The hotel is full. But the manager of this hotel can always say yes. And that's where things get strange.

Stick around, because this hotel has a bigger problem than a full lobby. It eventually runs out of room in a way no amount of building can fix.

**[INTRO]**

This is Hilbert's Hotel, a thought experiment created by the German mathematician David Hilbert in the early 1900s to explore the odd behavior of infinity.

The rules are simple. Every room has exactly one guest. There are no vacancies. So how does the manager fit one more person in?

The answer is to ask every guest to move. The guest in Room 1 moves to Room 2. The guest in Room 2 moves to Room 3. The guest in Room 3 moves to Room 4. This continues forever.

Every current guest still has a room. Room 1 is now empty, and the new arrival takes it.

It works because infinity doesn't run out. Shifting an infinite line of people forward by one spot never leaves anyone behind.

**[SEGMENT 1: THE BUSES]**

Now the hotel gets busy.

Suppose a single bus arrives with infinitely many passengers. The manager has a different trick for this. Move the guest in Room 1 to Room 2, Room 2 to Room 4, Room 3 to Room 6, and so on. Each guest moves to double their room number. Every even-numbered room is now filled by an existing guest.

That leaves all the odd-numbered rooms open. The bus passengers can fill them in order.

The manager is now handling an infinite bus with an infinite hotel. Fine. But what happens when infinitely many buses arrive, each carrying infinitely many people?

This is where the manager has to get clever. One method uses prime numbers. Each existing guest moves to a room whose number is a power of 2. The first bus fills rooms that are powers of 3. The second bus fills powers of 5. Every prime gets its own bus. Because every whole number can be broken down into primes in exactly one way, nobody is double-booked, and nobody gets left out.

The hotel handles infinitely many infinite buses. It is still not out of room.

**[SEGMENT 2: THE SEQUENCE PROBLEM]**

So far, the hotel has been fine. The manager has just needed the right scheme.

But the real trouble starts when we ask a different question. Not "how many guests are there?" but "how many different kinds of guests are there?"

Think about every guest as a decimal number. A guest could be 1, or 2.5, or 3.14159, or a number that never ends and never repeats. Mathematicians call these real numbers.

Are there more real numbers than whole numbers?

In 1874, Georg Cantor showed that yes, there are. His argument is one of the most famous in mathematics.

Suppose you try to make a list of all the real numbers between zero and one, one per room. You assign Room 1 a number, Room 2 another, and so on. Then build a new number by changing one digit in each guest's decimal expansion. Use the first digit of the first guest, the second digit of the second guest, and so on, and change each digit to something else.

This new number differs from the first guest in the first digit. It differs from the second guest in the second digit. It differs from every guest on the list somewhere.

So the new number isn't on your list. Your list was supposed to contain everyone. It didn't. No matter how you number the rooms, you can always find a guest who was left out.

**[SEGMENT 3: THE REAL PROBLEM]**

This is the point where the hotel truly runs out of room.

The manager can handle infinite buses. The manager can shift guests into prime-powered rooms. But no scheme that labels rooms with the counting numbers can ever seat every real number. The rooms are countable. The guests are not.

It is not a matter of building more rooms. You can add rooms labeled 1, 2, 3, and keep going forever, and it will still never be enough. The labeling system itself is the limit. Any list of rooms that goes one, two, three, and so on has gaps you can't close.

This is the difference between countable and uncountable infinity. Some infinities are larger than others. The whole numbers are an infinity, and the real numbers are a bigger one.

Hilbert's Hotel is a story about how infinity can be surprising and generous. It can take in more guests. But it has limits, and when the guests are uncountable, the hotel has no vacancy sign that can ever flip to "open."

**[OUTRO]**

So, how did an infinite hotel run out of room?

Not by running out of rooms. It ran out because the guests could not be lined up in a single file, no matter how long the line.
