**[SEGMENT 1: THE KISSING DEVICES]**

**HOST:** Okay, the concept is simple. You press your lips to a sensor pad. Pressure, movement, and sometimes warmth get sent over the internet to your partner's matching pad, which recreates it.

I borrowed two of these from a viewer (thank you, you know who you are) and tested them with my friend Dani, who agreed to be a good sport as long as nobody called it "a date."

**[B-ROLL: unboxing, pads on desk, Dani on video call]**

**HOST:** First impressions. The pad feels like a phone case designed by someone who has only heard kissing described to them. Squishy, slightly cold, and weirdly proud of itself.

**DANI (on call):** It's warming up. Oh no. It's warming up.

**HOST:** The honest result? The pressure sensing works better than I expected. You can tell when someone's pressing softly versus enthusiastically. But there's about a half-second lag, and in kissing, a half-second lag is basically a different language.

**[ON SCREEN: Rating card. Novelty 9/10. Romance 5/10. Lag 2/10.]**

**HOST:** Would I recommend it? As a couples' gimmick, absolutely. As a replacement for the real thing, no. Nothing replaces the real thing. But as a little "I'm thinking about you" ping, it's sweet. Which brings us to the thing that actually worked.

**[SEGMENT 2: THE FOUR-DOLLAR DATE]**

**HOST:** It's called Same Meal, Different Kitchen.

Step one: pick one recipe together. Keep it simple. Pasta, tacos, anything with fewer than eight ingredients.

Step two: shop separately, then hop on a video call and cook at the same time, propping your phone against a pepper grinder or a sad stack of cookbooks.

Step three: eat together, on camera, and rate each other's plating like a ruthless cooking-show judge.

**[B-ROLL: split-screen cooking, chaos, burned garlic]**

**HOST:** Total cost was four dollars, because I forgot the garlic and had to buy one. Then I burned it. But that's not the point. The point is you're not just watching each other do things. You're doing the same thing at the same time. That's the closeness people are actually chasing.

Pro tip: pick a playlist and press play at the exact same second. Silly, but it works.

**[SEGMENT 3: COMMENTS]**

**HOST:** Now, your comments.

First, from a viewer who goes by Moth Lamp: "How do you handle time zones without resenting each other?" Great question. Trade the inconvenience. One week you stay up late, the next week they wake up early. Fairness matters more than convenience.

Second, someone asked whether it's weird to keep a countdown to the next visit. Not weird at all. Countdowns are just hope with a calendar. Keep it.

And the one that made me put my coffee down: "Can you kiss through a video call without it being awkward?" Honestly? No. Nobody can. Embrace the awkwardness, laugh, and move on. The awkward part becomes the memory.
