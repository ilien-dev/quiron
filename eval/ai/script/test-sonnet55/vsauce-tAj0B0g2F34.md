**[SEGMENT 1: The Trick Explained, whiteboard]**

Let's break it down. Call your secret number *x*.

You double it: 2x. Add ten: 2x + 10. Halve it: x + 5. Then you subtract your original number, so x + 5 − x. The x cancels out, and you're left with five. Every time, for every number, even ones I couldn't guess.

That's the whole secret. A hidden variable walks in, does a little dance, and vanishes, leaving a constant behind. Most "math magic" is built on this idea: the audience controls the input, but you control the structure.

**[SEGMENT 2: The 1089 Trick]**

Okay, here's the one that always gets a reaction.

Write down any three-digit number where the first and last digits differ by at least two. Say, 825. Reverse it: 528. Subtract the smaller from the larger: 825 − 528 = 297. Now reverse *that*: 792. Add them together.

297 + 792 = 1089.

Try it with 631. Reverse is 136. Difference is 495. Reverse it: 594. Add them, and you get 1089 again. It never changes.

Here's why. Write your number as 100a + 10b + c. When you reverse it and subtract, the middle digit cancels, and you get 99 times (a − c). That means your difference is always a multiple of 99: 198, 297, 396, and so on up to 891. Every one of those has a middle digit of 9, and its outer digits add up to 9.

When you reverse and add, the ones place gives you 9 + 9... no, wait, let me be precise. The outer digits sum to 9, the middle digits give 18, and the carry works out so you always land on exactly 1089. It's tidy and a little bit eerie, and you can prove it with nothing more than place value.

Put it in a sealed envelope, have someone run the steps, and watch their face when you hand it over.
