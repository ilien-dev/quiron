**How to Catch a Dwarf Planet: Triton MM#3**

**[COLD OPEN, slow push-in on Neptune]**

NARRATOR: Neptune has a stolen moon. And today, we're going to figure out how it was stolen.

**[TITLE CARD]**

**[SCENE 1: Triton close-up]**

NARRATOR: Meet Triton. At 2,700 kilometers wide, it's bigger than Pluto. It's covered in nitrogen ice, sits at about minus 235 Celsius, and has geysers that Voyager 2 spotted in 1989.

But here's the weird part: Triton orbits *backwards*. Retrograde. Against Neptune's spin.

**[SCENE 2: Animation of moon formation]**

NARRATOR: Moons that form around their planet orbit the same direction it spins. Triton doesn't. That's a giveaway: it didn't form here. It's a Kuiper Belt object, a dwarf planet in all but name, that wandered too close and got caught.

**[SCENE 3: Capture problem diagram]**

NARRATOR: But catching something is harder than it sounds. An object falling toward Neptune speeds up, then flies right back out. To stay, it has to *lose energy*. So how?

**[SCENE 4: Three animated options]**

NARRATOR: Option one: gas drag. A young Neptune with a thick cloud of debris could slow Triton down. Option two: a collision with an existing moon. Option three, the current favorite: a binary exchange.

**[SCENE 5: Binary swap animation]**

NARRATOR: Imagine Triton traveling as part of a pair, like Pluto and Charon. As the pair passes Neptune, Neptune's gravity rips them apart. One partner is flung away, carrying off energy, and Triton is left behind, trapped.

**[SCENE 6: Aftermath]**

NARRATOR: The capture had violent consequences. Triton's new, stretched orbit would have been squeezed into a circle by tides, heating its interior and wrecking Neptune's original moons. Today only tiny Nereid hints at what once was.

**[SCENE 7: Future]**

NARRATOR: And the story isn't over. Triton is slowly spiraling inward. In a few billion years, it'll cross the Roche limit and shatter into a ring.

**[OUTRO]**

NARRATOR: So that's how you catch a dwarf planet. Next time, we'll talk about how we might go back. Thanks for watching.
