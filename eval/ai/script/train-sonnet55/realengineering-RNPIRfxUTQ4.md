**Why Do Wind Turbines Have Three Blades?**

*[Runtime ~3 min]*

---

**[COLD OPEN – wind turbines on a hillside, slow drone shot]**

Look at any modern wind farm and you'll notice something odd. Every turbine has exactly three blades. Not two. Not five. Three. Is that a coincidence? Not even close. Today, we'll find out why.

**[TITLE CARD]**

---

**[SECTION 1 – The Basic Job]**

A wind turbine has one job: pull energy out of moving air and turn it into electricity. The blades act like rotating wings. Wind flows over them, creates lift, and that lift spins a shaft connected to a generator.

So you might think more blades means more power. Sounds logical, right? But it doesn't work that way.

**[ANIMATION – airflow around a rotor]**

Each blade leaves turbulent, disturbed air behind it. If you add too many blades, the next blade runs into that messy air. Efficiency drops, and you've spent a lot more money on materials.

---

**[SECTION 2 – Why Not One or Two?]**

Fewer blades should be cheaper and more efficient, so why not just use one?

A single blade needs a heavy counterweight, and it has to spin extremely fast to capture the same energy. That causes noise, vibration, and a lot of stress on the structure.

Two blades are better, and some turbines have used them. But there's a catch.

**[ANIMATION – two-blade rotor turning to face the wind]**

When a turbine turns to face the wind, it's called yawing. With two blades, there's a moment when one blade points straight up and the other straight down, and the tower gets in the way of the lower one. Then the rotor is horizontal, and the balance shifts. This uneven loading shakes the machine with every rotation and wears out the bearings and gearbox.

Three blades solve this. The mass is spread evenly, so the rotor behaves almost like a solid disc. Forces stay balanced, and the turbine runs smoothly.

---

**[SECTION 3 – Why Not Four or More?]**

Going from two to three blades boosts efficiency by a few percent. Going from three to four adds only a tiny extra gain.

**[GRAPHIC – efficiency curve flattening out]**

That's the law of diminishing returns. Every blade is a huge piece of carbon fiber or fiberglass, sometimes over 80 meters long. Adding a fourth costs a fortune to build, ship, and install, for almost no extra power.

---

**[SECTION 4 – The Looks Factor]**

There's one more reason, and it's less technical. People tend to find three-bladed rotors more pleasing to look at. Two-bladed rotors can appear to flicker, and wind farms depend on public acceptance. Looks matter more than you'd think.

---

**[SECTION 5 – The Sweet Spot]**

So three blades is the sweet spot:

- Smooth and balanced
- Quiet enough for neighbors
- Efficient
- Affordable

Engineers did not guess. Decades of experiments, simulations, and real-world testing all pointed to the same answer.

**[CLOSING – sunset wind farm]**

Next time you drive past a wind farm, you'll know those three blades are the result of careful engineering and smart trade-offs.
