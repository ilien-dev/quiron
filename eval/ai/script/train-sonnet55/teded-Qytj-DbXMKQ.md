# How Thor Got His Hammer
*Video script, about 711 words of narration*

---

**[COLD OPEN: slow zoom on a glowing hammer, thunder rumbling]**

**NARRATOR:** Thor, the Norse god of thunder, is rarely seen without his hammer, Mjölnir. It can level mountains, call down lightning, and fly back to his hand every time it's thrown. But the most powerful weapon in the nine worlds wasn't born in a moment of glory. It began with a prank, a reckless bet, and a very bad haircut.

**[TITLE CARD]**

---

**[SCENE 1: THE HALLS OF ASGARD]**

**NARRATOR:** One morning in Asgard, Thor's wife Sif wakes to find that her famous golden hair is gone, cut off down to the scalp. Thor knows at once who's responsible. Only one god would think this was funny: Loki, the trickster. Thor finds him, seizes him, and swears to break every bone in his body.

Loki, always quick on his feet, pleads for mercy. He promises to travel to Svartalfheim, the underground home of the dwarves, and have their master craftsmen make Sif new hair, golden hair that will grow like the real thing. Thor lets him go, but he's watching.

---

**[SCENE 2: THE FORGES OF THE DWARVES]**

**NARRATOR:** Deep beneath the earth, Loki finds the sons of Ivaldi, the finest smiths he knows. They forge the golden hair, and it's perfect. But they don't stop there. They also build Skidbladnir, a ship that sails on any water and folds small enough to fit in a pocket, and Gungnir, a spear that never misses its target.

Any other god would be satisfied. But Loki has never been able to leave well enough alone. He seeks out two other dwarves, the brothers Brokkr and Sindri, and boasts that their work could never match this. Then he bets his own head on it.

---

**[SCENE 3: THE BET]**

**NARRATOR:** Sindri accepts. He lays a pig's skin in the forge and tells his brother, "Whatever happens, do not stop pumping the bellows."

Loki, realizing he might actually lose, turns into a fly and bites Brokkr's hand. Brokkr grits his teeth and keeps pumping. When Sindri opens the forge, out leaps Gullinbursti, a boar with bristles of living gold that glow in the dark and race across sky and sea.

Next, Sindri puts gold into the fire. The fly bites Brokkr's neck, harder this time. Brokkr endures it. From the forge comes Draupnir, a gold ring that, every ninth night, drops eight new rings just like itself.

For the third treasure, Sindri places a lump of iron in the flames. "If the bellows stop," he warns, "everything is ruined." Loki, desperate, bites Brokkr on the eyelid. Blood runs into his eyes, and for one moment Brokkr lets go to wipe it away. The fire sputters. Sindri pulls out a hammer, magnificent, but with a handle just a little too short.

---

**[SCENE 4: THE JUDGMENT OF THE GODS]**

**NARRATOR:** Now the gods gather to judge: Odin, Thor, and Freyr. Loki presents his treasures. Odin receives the spear Gungnir, Freyr the ship, and Thor the golden hair for Sif, which, as promised, takes root and grows. Then Brokkr presents his. Odin gets the ring Draupnir, Freyr the golden boar, and Thor receives the hammer.

Brokkr explains its powers. It can strike anything and never miss. It always returns to its owner's hand. And it can shrink small enough to hide inside Thor's tunic.

Loki argues that the handle is too short, and the gods agree it's a flaw. But they also agree the hammer is the finest of all the treasures, because with it, Asgard can defend itself against the giants. Brokkr wins the bet.

---

**[SCENE 5: LOKI'S ESCAPE]**

**NARRATOR:** Loki bolts, but Thor drags him back. Brokkr moves to claim his prize, and Loki, ever the trickster, argues: "I promised you my head, not my neck. You can't take one without the other."

Brokkr, outwitted, can't collect. Instead, he sews Loki's lips shut. A lesson, at least for a while.

---

**[CLOSING: Thor raising Mjölnir against a stormy sky]**

**NARRATOR:** So the same god who ruined Sif's beauty ended up arming the gods. Loki's mischief caused the problem, his scheming produced the treasures, and even his last-second interference gave the hammer its famous flaw. In Norse myth, chaos and creation are never far apart. And the gods' greatest defense was forged, in part, by their greatest troublemaker.
