# The World War II Meme That Circled the World

**[COLD OPEN]**

Long before the internet, one meme went viral without a single share button. It showed up on tanks, bomb casings, and bathroom walls from Normandy to the Pacific. Just three words: *Kilroy was here.*

**[TITLE CARD]**

**[SECTION 1: THE DOODLE]**

You've probably seen it. A bald little head peeks over a wall, with a long nose drooping over the edge and fingers gripping the top. Underneath, the phrase that American soldiers scrawled everywhere they went.

The funny part is that nobody quite agrees on where it started.

**[SECTION 2: THE MAN BEHIND THE NAME]**

The most popular explanation leads to Quincy, Massachusetts. James J. Kilroy was a ship inspector at the Fore River Shipyard. To show he'd checked a rivet job, he chalked "Kilroy was here" on the steel. Those marks were often sealed inside the hulls, so when sailors later found the words in hidden compartments, it seemed as though some mysterious man had been everywhere before them.

In 1946, the American Transit Association held a radio contest to find the real Kilroy. James Kilroy brought shipyard officials as witnesses, and he won. His prize was a full-sized trolley car, which he reportedly turned into a playhouse for his nine children.

**[SECTION 3: THE DRAWING]**

Here's the twist. The doodle and the phrase probably began separately. In Britain, the same peeking face was called "Mr. Chad," usually grumbling about wartime shortages: *"Wot, no tea?"* Somewhere along the way, American and British troops merged the two.

**[SECTION 4: THE LEGENDS]**

As the joke spread, so did the stories. One tale claims Stalin left a VIP bathroom at the Potsdam Conference asking, "Who is Kilroy?" Others say Kilroy appeared on the Arc de Triomphe and even on the Moon. There's no evidence for most of these, but they show how big the joke became.

One sighting is definitely real. Kilroy is engraved at the National World War II Memorial in Washington, D.C., hidden in two spots for visitors to find.
