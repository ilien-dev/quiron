# Top 10 Facts: The Simpsons

**[COLD OPEN]**

**NARRATOR:**
For more than three decades, one yellow family has lived in our living rooms. They've survived nuclear meltdowns, alien abductions, and roughly a thousand of Homer's terrible ideas. But how well do you really know The Simpsons? Today we're counting down the top ten facts about the longest-running scripted show in American primetime history. Grab a donut. Let's go.

**#10: Created in a Waiting Room**

The Simpsons was born in about fifteen minutes. In 1987, cartoonist Matt Groening was waiting in the lobby of producer James L. Brooks's office. He'd been invited to pitch his comic strip, *Life in Hell*, as animated shorts. Then he realized that adapting it would mean giving up the rights to his own work. So, right there in the lobby, he sketched a brand-new family.

He named them after his own relatives. His father was Homer, his mother was Margaret, and his sisters were Lisa and Maggie. For the son, he didn't use his own name. He chose "Bart," an anagram of "brat."

---

**#9: They Started as Filler**

Before they had their own show, the Simpsons were bumpers on *The Tracey Ullman Show*. Starting in April 1987, short crudely animated segments ran between sketches. The characters looked rough, and Homer sounded more like Walter Matthau than the Homer we know today.

The shorts became so popular that Fox gave the family a half-hour series. It premiered on December 17, 1989, with a Christmas special called "Simpsons Roasting on an Open Fire." That episode also introduced the family dog, Santa's Little Helper.

---

**#8: Why Is Everyone Yellow?**

The answer is surprisingly practical. Groening wanted the show to stand out to viewers flipping through channels. He reasoned that if someone saw a flash of bright yellow on screen, they'd instantly know they had landed on The Simpsons.

The choice also solved a design problem. The animators found that the characters' hair looked strange in skin tones, so Bart's and Lisa's spiky heads simply became part of their yellow skin.

---

**#7: The Secret of Springfield**

For decades, the writers deliberately hid which state Springfield is in. The show is full of contradictory clues and jokes that cut off just before a state name is revealed. Springfield was chosen because it's one of the most common town names in America.

In 2012, Groening finally admitted that the town was inspired by Springfield, Oregon. He picked it because it was the setting of the classic sitcom *Father Knows Best*. Even so, the show's official Springfield has no state, and the writers like it that way.

---

**#6: "D'oh!" Is in the Dictionary**

Homer's famous catchphrase never appeared in the scripts. The writers simply typed "annoyed grunt." Voice actor Dan Castellaneta borrowed the sound from James Finlayson, a Scottish actor in old Laurel and Hardy films, who used a drawn-out "Dooooh!" Castellaneta sped it up, and an icon was born.

In 2001, "D'oh" was added to the Oxford English Dictionary, defined as an exclamation used to comment on a foolish action. Not bad for a stage direction.

---

**#5: The Voices Almost Swapped**

Nancy Cartwright, the voice of Bart, originally came in to audition for Lisa. When she saw the description of Bart, she asked to read for him instead and got the part on the spot.

Meanwhile, Yeardley Smith, the voice of Lisa, first auditioned for Bart. The producers felt her voice sounded too much like a girl's, so she was cast as Lisa. Cartwright also voices Nelson, Ralph Wiggum, and Todd Flanders. The main cast members have each played dozens of characters over the years.

---

**#4: Maggie's First Word Cost a Fortune**

Maggie almost never speaks, which made her first word a big moment. In the 1992 episode "Lisa's First Word," Maggie says "Daddy" after Homer has put her to bed. That single word was voiced by Hollywood legend Elizabeth Taylor.

According to show lore, Taylor recorded the line again and again until the producers were satisfied. It may be the most expensive one-word performance in television history.

---

**#3: A Pop Star in Disguise**

In the 1991 episode "Stark Raving Dad," Homer is sent to a mental institution and meets a large man who believes he's Michael Jackson. The speaking voice really was Michael Jackson. He was credited under the pseudonym "John Jay Smith" for contract reasons.

Because of those contract issues, the singing was performed by a sound-alike, Kipp Lennon. Jackson also co-wrote the song "Happy Birthday Lisa" with Bart. In 2019, the producers pulled the episode from circulation.

---

**#2: The Show That Predicts the Future**

The Simpsons has a reputation for seeing the future. In 2000, the episode "Bart to the Future" showed Lisa as president, inheriting a budget crisis from President Trump. In 1998, a background gag referred to "20th Century Fox, a division of Walt Disney Co." Disney actually bought Fox two decades later.

The writers usually explain that this isn't prophecy. When you've made more than seven hundred episodes and joked about almost everything, some of those jokes are bound to come true eventually.

---

**#1: A Record Nobody Can Touch**

The Simpsons is the longest-running American scripted primetime series. In 2018, it broke the record held by the western *Gunsmoke* when it aired its 636th episode. The show has since been renewed well into its fourth decade, with seasons planned through the end of the 2020s.

It has also expanded into the real world. *The Simpsons Movie* made more than half a billion dollars worldwide in 2007. To promote it, 7-Eleven converted a dozen U.S. stores into real Kwik-E-Marts that sold Squishees and Buzz Cola. The show has won dozens of Emmys and holds the Guinness World Record for the most guest stars in a TV series. It also has a star on the Hollywood Walk of Fame.

---

**[BONUS FACT]**

Homer's middle name is Jay. Groening chose it as a tribute to Bullwinkle J. Moose and Rocky the Flying Squirrel, who both took their middle initial from their creator, Jay Ward.
