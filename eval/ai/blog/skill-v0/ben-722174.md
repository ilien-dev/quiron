# CodeLand 2021: Motivation & career-long connections for early-career devs

Breaking into tech can feel like an uphill battle, especially when you're just starting out and don't have a big network to lean on. That's why conferences like CodeLand exist, and it's why the 2021 edition was worth so much to early-career devs who needed motivation and people to know.

## What is CodeLand?

CodeLand is put on by freeCodeCamp, and it was designed with newer devs in mind. A lot of tech conferences assume you have years of experience and know every niche tool. CodeLand is built to be accessible, in what it covers and in what it costs. The talks are easy to follow, the speakers come from all kinds of backgrounds, and the whole thing is far less intimidating than your typical industry conference.

If you've just landed your first dev job, or if you are still job hunting, that matters. It is easy to feel like an imposter when the people around you keep casually dropping the names of obscure frameworks or bragging about their FAANG interviews. CodeLand strips a lot of that away.

## Motivation that actually sticks

One of the biggest things I took away from CodeLand 2021 was how much motivation you can get from other people's stories. A lot of the speakers weren't giving flashy technical deep-dives. They were telling the story of how they got into tech, and the messy parts were left in.

Hearing someone talk about being rejected from dozens of jobs before landing their first role, or about switching careers in their thirties to become a dev, hits differently than a motivational quote on LinkedIn does. It's real. It reminds you that you are not the only one who is struggling right now, and that the struggle is not a sign you don't belong.

That kind of motivation is hard to make on your own. You can read all the "you got this" tweets you want. It is still different when you hear it from someone who was where you are.

## Building career-long connections

The other big piece of CodeLand is networking. I don't mean the stiff kind where you swap business cards. The conference is virtual and run by the community, so the connections that form tend to be more organic. People chat in the conference Discord, comment on talks, and follow up with each other after it ends.

For early-career devs this matters a lot, because the people you know early on often decide what comes your way later: job referrals, mentorship, side projects to work on together, or just someone to vent to when things get hard. CodeLand gives you a low-pressure place to start on that without the awkwardness of an in-person mixer.

I walked away with a handful of new connections. I still talk to these people. We trade advice, and now and then we work on small projects together. None of that came from a polished elevator pitch; it came from actually talking with people who were dealing with the same things I was.

## Why this matters for early-career devs

When you're early in your career, it's easy to think your technical skill is the only thing that matters. Learn enough JavaScript, grind enough LeetCode problems, build enough projects, and the job will come. But community and the people you know play a massive role in how careers actually unfold.

Conferences like CodeLand exist to remind you of that. They give you mentors, peers, and a sense of belonging that is hard to find when you code alone in your room. And since it's aimed at newer devs, you're not fighting for attention with a room full of senior engineers who already have their networks figured out.

## Final thoughts

If you missed CodeLand 2021, keep an eye out for the next one, or for other conferences run by and for the community. The technical content is good, sure. For me the motivation and the people were the best part.

Learning syntax is only part of it. You also need to find your people, the ones who remind you that everyone struggles at the start. CodeLand gave me all of that.
