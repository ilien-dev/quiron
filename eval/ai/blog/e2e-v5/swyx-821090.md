# September 11th from Singapore

It's been 20 years since 9/11. I was in Singapore when it happened, about 12 hours ahead of New York, so for us it was night. My memory of it is patchy: very clear on the moment itself, and then very little after.

## The moment

I was ending a night class at home, and the TV in the living room was on. First thought when the first tower was burning: an accident. The second plane changed that. Then the Pentagon, then the fourth plane. The local news reporters were as lost as the rest of us.

The other thing that stuck is my baby sister in her playpen, completely unaware of any of it. Same feeling about two years later during [SARS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%932004_SARS_outbreak), with a little kid in the room who had no idea.

There were no American friends in my life back then, so everything I know about the day itself was passed on to me later, as stories:

- [The Falling Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Falling_Man)
- the [phone calls from Flight 93](https://www.nps.gov/flni/learn/historyculture/phone-calls-from-flight-93.htm)
- the firefighters [raising the flag at Ground Zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raising_the_Flag_at_Ground_Zero)

## What I don't remember

How my parents reacted, or my teachers, is gone. And at the time I wasn't aware of how the response shifted from Afghanistan to Iraq.

## What changed in Singapore

Singapore is about 22% Muslim, and our neighbours are Muslim-majority countries. So the work here over the next 1 to 7 years was about preempting copycats and rebuilding interfaith ties (the Straits Times has a [timeline of 20 years of securing Singapore after 9/11](https://www.straitstimes.com/singapore/politics/timeline-20-years-of-securing-singapore-following-911-attacks) if you want the details).

My first trip abroad was about 4 years later, for a choir competition. By then the [shoe rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_shoe_bomb_attempt) was already in place, but the [liquids ban](https://www.mentalfloss.com/article/565368/tsa-airport-liquids-rule-water-bottles-explained) hadn't been introduced yet. For a sense of how much airports changed, watch [Rachel walking to her flight 30 minutes before it leaves](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g1q4w4wq8Cw).

## The school debates

In school we debated End of History vs Clash of Civilizations. Everyone had strong opinions, and very few of my classmates had actually read either. Honestly I doubt those debates had much value.

Noah Smith's take is that [the 9/11 era is over](https://noahpinion.substack.com/p/the-911-era-is-over-good): state power won, once citizens gave up their privacy for it.

## What I'd do differently

A few regrets, 20 years on. Not journaling, for one: this post would be a lot less patchy if I had. Having no close Muslim friends to talk it through with is another. And back then the difference between sudden and chronic disasters didn't register with me.
