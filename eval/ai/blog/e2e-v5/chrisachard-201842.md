# How to Find Consulting Clients

The #1 question I get about consulting is: "how do you find clients?"

I get it - I struggled with the same thing when I was starting out, so here's what has actually worked for me.

## Meet people in your field

Most consulting contracts come from people you know, so the best thing you can do to find clients is to meet more people in your field.

I don't mean "networking" in the business-card sense; I mean showing up where other developers in your field already are. Local language meetups are great for this: go to a few, then give a lightning talk, and a few months later give a full talk.

## Have public proof that you know your stuff

People need some way to see that you're an expert before they'll pay you like one. That can be a portfolio, a blog, an ebook, a YouTube channel, or a podcast.

Ebooks work great for this, by the way.

## Tell people you're looking

This one sounds obvious, but a lot of people skip it: tell your contacts that you're looking for consulting work!

And when you ask, don't ask whether *their* company needs consulting help - ask who they know who might need consulting help. Most of my past clients have been friends-of-friends - people I never would have reached on my own.

Also watch for hiring posts in your network. When someone you know is hiring, that's a chance to pitch contracting instead. It might not pay off right away... sometimes it's months or even years later.

## Subcontract for consultancies

Other consulting shops are a good source of work too. You'll make less than you would with your own clients, but you also deal with fewer hassles.

I'd target smaller shops, around 5-10 people. Many of them have overflow work they need help with.

## If you need work fast

Skip the big freelance marketplaces, and instead:

- Look at tech job boards
- Find a job you could do, and email the hiring manager directly (not HR)
- Offer to do the work as a contract instead of full-time

Smaller companies are best for this because there's less red tape. Plenty of companies will ignore you or say no, but honestly, I've been surprised by how many companies say yes!

When someone does say no, ask if they know anyone else who might need help. The tech world is small, and a "no" can still turn into a referral.

## You don't need that many

Consulting isn't volume sales, and you're not trying to close lots of deals - a few good consulting clients can make for a really good year.
