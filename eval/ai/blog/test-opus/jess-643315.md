# The 7 Most Popular DEV Posts from the Past Week

Welcome back to our weekly roundup of the most popular posts on DEV! Every week, we look at the articles that got the community talking, reacting, and sharing. Whether you missed them the first time or want to revisit a favorite, here are the seven posts that stood out this week.

## 1. Stop Writing Clever Code

Kicking off this week's list is a thoughtful reminder that readable code beats clever code almost every time. The author walks through real examples from their own codebase where a "smart" one-liner turned into a debugging nightmare months later, and shows how they rewrote each one to be clearer. The comments turned into a great discussion about where the line between concise and cryptic really is.

> "Code is read far more often than it's written. Optimize for the reader."

## 2. I Built a Full-Stack App in a Weekend. Here's What I Learned

This one struck a chord with anyone who loves a good side project. The author documents 48 hours of building, from picking a stack to deploying, including the parts that went wrong. It's honest, practical, and full of small tips about scoping, tooling, and knowing when to call something done.

## 3. A Beginner's Guide to Understanding Async/Await

Async code trips up a lot of developers, especially early on. This post breaks down promises, `async`, and `await` using simple analogies and short examples, then builds up to handling errors and running tasks in parallel. If you've ever been confused about why your `console.log` runs before your data arrives, this is the post for you.

## 4. What Nobody Tells You About Your First Tech Job

This heartfelt post shares lessons from the first year in a developer role: asking questions without fear, dealing with imposter syndrome, and learning to navigate a large codebase. Lots of community members chimed in with their own first-job stories, which made the comment section just as valuable as the article itself.

## 5. CSS Tricks I Wish I Knew Sooner

A fun, visual post packed with modern CSS techniques, including `clamp()` for fluid typography, `:has()` for parent selection, and container queries for truly responsive components. Each tip comes with a small demo you can try right away. Expect to bookmark this one.

## 6. How We Cut Our Build Times in Half

A detailed case study from a team that was tired of waiting on slow CI pipelines. The author explains how they profiled their builds, introduced caching, parallelized tests, and removed unused dependencies. The before-and-after numbers are impressive, and the approach is easy to apply to your own projects.

## 7. Let's Talk About Burnout

Closing out the list is an important and honest conversation about burnout in tech. The author shares their own experience, the warning signs they missed, and the changes that helped them recover. It's a reminder to take care of yourself and to look out for your teammates, too.

## That's a Wrap!

Congratulations to all of this week's authors, and thank you to everyone who read, reacted, and left thoughtful comments. The DEV community is what it is because of people like you sharing what you know.

Have a post you think deserves a spot next week? Share it in the comments below, and don't forget to follow the tags you care about so you never miss a great read. See you next week!
