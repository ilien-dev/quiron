# My 2020 Year in Review

What a year. When I sat down to write this, I scrolled back through my calendar, my GitHub activity, and my notes app to piece together what actually happened in 2020. So much of it blurred together that I'm glad I kept some kind of record. This post is partly for you and partly for future me, so I can remember what this strange year looked like from the inside.

Let's get into it.

## The Big Picture

At the start of 2020, I had a list of goals that looked pretty normal: speak at a couple of conferences, ship a side project, read more books, and travel a bit. By March, most of that list was out the window.

Like a lot of people, I went from commuting to an office to working from my kitchen table almost overnight. The first few weeks were a mix of stress, uncertainty, and trying to figure out how to take meetings while my neighbor's dog barked through the wall.

But as the year went on, I adjusted, and a lot of good things happened too. Here's how it broke down.

## Work

### Going fully remote

Our team went fully remote in March and never went back. It took a while to find a rhythm. Early on, we had way too many video calls. We were trying to recreate the office on Zoom, and it was exhausting.

Over time, we moved toward more asynchronous communication: written updates, recorded demos, and thoughtful pull request descriptions. That shift was one of the best changes we made all year. It gave everyone more focused time, and it helped teammates in different time zones feel less left out.

### Projects I worked on

The biggest project for me this year was helping migrate a large part of our frontend to TypeScript. It was a slow, incremental process. We turned on `allowJs`, converted files one at a time, and gradually tightened the compiler settings. By the end of the year, the majority of our frontend code was typed, and the number of "undefined is not a function" bugs in production dropped noticeably.

I also spent a good chunk of time on accessibility work, auditing components, fixing keyboard navigation, and improving how we handled focus in modals. It's the kind of work that doesn't always get noticed, but it's some of the work I'm proudest of.

### Mentoring

I mentored two junior developers this year, both of whom started their jobs remotely. That's a tough way to begin a career. We did regular pairing sessions, and I learned a lot about explaining things clearly over a screen share. Watching them grow in confidence over the year was genuinely one of the highlights of 2020 for me.

## Community

### Writing

I set a goal to write one blog post a month and I... mostly hit it. I published ten posts this year, here on DEV and on my own blog. The ones that did best weren't the ones I expected. A quick post about a VS Code setting I use every day got far more attention than a long deep-dive I spent weeks on. Lesson learned: small, practical posts are valuable, and I shouldn't wait for everything to be perfect before publishing.

### Speaking

All of my in-person talks were cancelled, but I ended up giving three virtual talks and joining a couple of podcast episodes. Speaking to a webcam with no audience reactions is a very strange experience. You have no idea whether a joke landed. Still, virtual events meant people could attend from anywhere, and I got questions from folks in countries I'd never have been able to visit.

### Live streaming

This was new for me. Around the middle of the year, I started streaming some of my coding sessions. At first, it was just me and two viewers, one of whom was my partner. But over the months, a small, kind community formed around it. Coding in public is a great way to learn, because people point out things you'd never notice on your own, and it also keeps you honest about actually showing up.

### Open source

I made my first meaningful contributions to a couple of open source projects this year, including a documentation overhaul and a few bug fixes. I also maintained one of my own small libraries, closing old issues and cutting a new major release. Hacktoberfest brought a wave of pull requests, some great and some less great, and taught me a lot about writing better contribution guidelines.

## Learning

Here are a few things I spent time learning in 2020:

- **TypeScript**, in much more depth than before, thanks to the migration at work
- **Testing Library**, which changed the way I think about testing UI
- **Rust**, at least the basics. I worked through a good part of the official book and built a tiny CLI tool.
- **Writing**, which I think is an underrated developer skill

I also read more books than I have in years, both technical and non-technical. Being home a lot will do that.

## Personal

Outside of tech, 2020 was about slowing down. I cooked a lot more, started going for long walks every day, and picked up a guitar again after years of it sitting in the corner.

It wasn't all easy. There were weeks where I felt disconnected and anxious, and where the lines between work and home disappeared completely. I got better at setting boundaries: closing my laptop at a set time, turning off Slack notifications on my phone, and actually taking time off even when there was nowhere to go.

If you struggled this year, you're not alone. Please be kind to yourself.

## What Went Well

- Adapting to remote work, and helping my team do the same
- Keeping up with writing
- Starting to stream and building a small community
- Mentoring, which was far more rewarding than I expected

## What Didn't Go So Well

- I never shipped the side project I planned. It's still sitting in a private repo.
- I let burnout creep up on me in the fall before I noticed it.
- I said yes to too many things and spread myself too thin at times.

## Looking Ahead to 2021

For next year, I want to keep it simple:

1. **Keep writing**, and focus on posts that help people solve real problems.
2. **Stream consistently**, with a regular schedule.
3. **Ship that side project**, even if it's smaller than I originally planned.
4. **Protect my time** and say no more often.
5. **Take real breaks**, whatever that looks like.

## Thank You

Finally, a huge thank you to everyone who read my posts, joined a stream, left a comment, or reached out this year. The developer community has been a real source of support in a year where many of us needed it.

How was your 2020? I'd love to read your year in review too, so drop a link in the comments. Here's to a better 2021 for all of us!
