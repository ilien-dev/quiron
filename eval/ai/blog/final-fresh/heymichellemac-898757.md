# The 7 Step Process I Used To Build A Landing Page To Promote My Book

I built a custom landing page for my eBook, [Design Faster, Design Better](https://designfaster.netlify.app/). The page has two jobs: drive sales, and explain to people what the book actually covers.

More and more creators are ditching the default Gumroad product page for a page of their own, and I wanted to do the same. The process I followed has seven steps and costs nothing. Mine needed some HTML, CSS and Tailwind, but you don't have to code to follow it, because there are no-code routes too and I'll list a few of them in step 5.

---

## Step 1: Define Your Goal

I've skipped this step before and regretted all the time I wasted, so don't skip it.

Give each page one goal. Mine was to promote the book and let people buy it, at a low cost, and I also wanted to build the page by hand, because it was a chance to practice my web development skills.

---

## Step 2: Know Your Target Audience

Who is the page for? The audience I settled on is people who want to get better at design and do it productively, and I kept that broad on purpose.

If you're not sure how to pin down your own audience, here are a few resources:

- [Marketing Evolution](https://www.marketingevolution.com/marketing-essentials/target-audience)
- [HubSpot](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/target-audience)
- [Single Mind Consulting](https://www.singlemindconsulting.com/blog/ux-identifying-your-target-audience/)

---

## Step 3: Write The Copy

Write your copy before you design anything. The words decide what the page needs, so if you design first you end up squeezing text into boxes that were never made for it.

Copywriting is still my weak spot. Most of what I know about it I picked up on Twitter, from people like [Jeremy Moser](https://twitter.com/jmoserr), [Charles Floate](https://twitter.com/Charles_SEO), [Alex Garcia](https://twitter.com/alexgarcia_atx) and [Nicolas Cole](https://twitter.com/Nicolascole77).

Here's the draft I worked from:

1. **Hero title:** "Create Brilliant Designs In Half The Time", with a "Get My Copy" button
2. **Intro:** the pain point, and how the book fixes it
3. **Chapter breakdown:** what's in each part of the book
4. **Free sample:** a download so people can try before they buy
5. **Final call to action**

Read your copy out loud. If it sounds like a corporate brochure, keep rewriting until it sounds like you're explaining the book to a friend.

---

## Step 4: Find Design Inspiration

For layouts and visual ideas, I looked at these accounts on Twitter: [@luisthewiz](https://twitter.com/luisthewiz), [@LoftedLearning](https://twitter.com/LoftedLearning), [@camduncannnn](https://twitter.com/camduncannnn) and [@tailwindui](https://twitter.com/tailwindui). ConvertKit also has a page of [product landing page examples](https://convertkit.com/product-landing-pages) that's worth a scroll.

Because the copy already existed, the layout mostly followed from it. Each section of text became a section of the page.

---

## Step 5: Pick Your Tools

I built mine with [Tailwind](https://tailwindcss.com/) on top of plain HTML and CSS. The page is hosted for free on [Netlify](https://www.netlify.com/), on a Netlify subdomain for now.

If you don't want to code, you have options:

- templates from [ThemeForest](https://themeforest.net/) or [Gumroad](https://gumroad.com/)
- [Carrd](https://carrd.co/), which is the best choice if you're completely clueless about building websites
- [Webflow](https://webflow.com/)
- [fromNotion](https://fromnotion.com/)

My opinion: use what you already know. The goal is a page that sells the book, and learning a new tool can wait.

---

## Step 6: Test It

Before launch, check the page on your phone, tablet and computer. Get friends to look at it too, and proofread everything before it goes out. They'll catch things you've stopped seeing because you're too close to the project.

---

## Step 7: Hit Publish

This was the hardest step for me. Every time I looked at the page I found another tweak to make or another typo to fix.

Set yourself a realistic launch date. The page doesn't have to be finished to go live, and mine still isn't: it's missing testimonials and reviews, and I want to put the book on Amazon through KDP and link to it from the page. I keep a running list of improvements for all of that.
