# How I Am Getting Out From Under My Student Debt

Before anything else, I'm not a financial advisor and I'm not especially well studied on the subject. Everything here was learned from reading online, from a friend and from a lot of trial and error. This is what I'm doing with my own debt, not financial advice.

## How I got here

I spent 5 years in college in Portland, Oregon. The first ~2 were at a community college, where I was figuring things out and working toward a Transfer Associate degree, and the rest were at Oregon Institute of Technology, where I earned a Bachelor of Software Engineering.

The transfer is where things went sideways. I was told by my school that transferring in Winter was fine. It wasn't. The courses cycle starting in Fall and I lost about a year waiting for the classes I needed to come back around.

At the start of my fifth year I ran out of money, and a friend cosigned a big private loan so I could finish. My part-time jobs never covered my costs, and I made some bad credit card decisions along the way.

When I graduated in May 2018 I had just over $17k in credit card debt and around $93k in college debt, $12k of which was a high-interest private loan.

## Doing the math

I don't have kids and my living costs are split with my partner, which leaves me roughly $1,800 a month to put toward debt. My first goal was simple division:

**110,000 / 1,800 = 61.11 months, or about 5.1 years**

That ignores interest. Big thing to ignore! If there's one tip I'd give anyone it's to pay more than the minimum. On a high-interest balance the minimum barely touches the principal and the balance can keep growing while you pay it.

To keep track of all of it I expanded a budget spreadsheet I'd set up before and barely used. This time I actually kept it up.

## Snowball or avalanche?

The two methods I kept running into were the [debt snowball](https://www.daveramsey.com/blog/how-the-debt-snowball-method-works), which Dave Ramsey recommends (smallest balance first, for quick wins), and the [debt avalanche](https://www.nerdwallet.com/blog/finance/what-is-a-debt-avalanche/) as Nerd Wallet describes it (highest interest rate first). I chose the avalanche, since it saves more on interest and I was fine with the wins coming slowly.

## Credit cards, then the private loan

That meant the credit cards went first, and I paid only the minimums on my student loans while I worked on them. As the card balances came down my credit score went up, which let me move balances onto 0% APR offers that lasted 12 to 18 months. By early 2019 I had no interest-bearing credit card debt left.

Next up was the private loan. Its rate was 11.13% and it grew by $1-2k while I was only paying the minimum. It was paid off in June 2019 with the help of a few more 0% balance transfers.

## Where I am now

My living costs turned out to be lower than I expected, so I set a new goal: pay everything off in 3 years, interest included. I'll have paid about $35k by the end of the year, and my spreadsheet currently forecasts 2.9 years.

A couple of caveats before you copy any of this:

- If you use 0% promotional offers, don't let them lapse. Once the promo period ends, the interest comes back.
- Consolidation didn't suit me, but it's a perfectly valid option and worth looking into for your own situation.
