# How I Handle the Fear of Rejection

This is a lightly edited version of my November newsletter email, which was also my [first one](https://narvi-software-llc.ck.page/674caf55d4). I wrote it in a coffee shop right before RubyConf, with [2112 by Rush](https://www.youtube.com/watch?time_continue=180&v=w5jwxrTqoEA&feature=emb_logo) playing.

I got a Twitter DM from a veteran of the game industry. He has spent 20 years there, including stints at Blizzard and Twitch, and he works remotely from Portugal. Esports have been rough lately, so he's job hunting. He wants a technical role (he started his career as a SysAdmin), and as a hobby he runs a hosting service at cost. I think he would fit well on an SRE team, so if your company is hiring for one, DM me.

What's holding him back is that he's afraid of rejection. I have given the same advice on this about 100 times and never written it down, so here it is.

## Cold showers

Something most people don't realize about me: I have social anxiety, and I'm very introverted. People often mistake me for an extrovert because I push networking and transparency so hard.

Here is a weird example of how I approach things that scare me: I take cold showers. When I began, I did 30 seconds of cold water and added 10 seconds per day, and now three or four minutes is easy. It's homemade exposure therapy.

Rejection works the same way. Years ago I did cold calls and a lot of LinkedIn outreach, and after a few dozen rejections each additional one stung less. I wrote about that period [here on DEV](https://dev.to/jacobherrington/how-i-got-hired-at-dev-and-every-other-tech-job-10hf).

It still stings a bit, but it stops being the thing that decides whether you try.

## Job requirements are a wish list

If you are scared to apply for a job, look at the "requirements" section again. I have seen that section as an applicant and as a hiring engineer. It is a wish list. These days I mostly skip it.

Something like 90% of applicants don't meet the requirements. (Disclaimer: I invented that stat.) I've probably never met the requirements for any tech job I've held.

## The worst case is a "no"

This goes way beyond jobs. It applies to dates, clubs, promotions and raises too. The worst case is that someone says no, and a no is more practice.

Everyone gets told no. The people who keep going have just heard it enough times that it stopped being scary.
