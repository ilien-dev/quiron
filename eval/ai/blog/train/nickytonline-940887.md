# My 2021 Year in Review

Every year I tell myself I'll write one of these, and every year I almost don't. But looking back is one of the best ways I know to figure out where I want to go next, so here we are. 2021 was a year of change, learning, and a fair bit of reflection. Let's get into it.

## Work

The biggest thing that happened this year was a job change. After a few years at my previous company, I decided it was time for something new. It wasn't an easy decision. I liked my team and I'd learned a lot, but I felt like I'd plateaued, and I wanted to work on something that pushed me in new directions.

The interview process was, well, an interview process. Some companies had great experiences with thoughtful take-homes and friendly conversations. Others had multiple rounds of whiteboard puzzles that had nothing to do with the job. I learned a lot about what I value in a team just from how companies ran their hiring.

I ended up joining a team where I get to work more on developer experience and tooling, which is something I've always loved. The first few months were a mix of excitement and imposter syndrome, which I think is pretty normal when you join a new place. Getting up to speed on a new codebase, new people, and new processes all at once is a lot. But by the end of the year, I felt like I'd found my footing.

A few highlights from work:

- Shipped my first major feature at the new job, which involved a pretty big refactor of how we handle configuration
- Helped improve our CI pipeline, cutting build times noticeably
- Started pairing regularly with teammates, which has been one of the best ways to learn the codebase and build relationships

## Learning

I spent a lot of this year going deeper rather than wider. Instead of jumping on every new framework, I focused on getting better at the things I already use.

**TypeScript** was a big one. I've used it for a while, but this year I finally got comfortable with some of the more advanced type features: generics that actually make sense, conditional types, and template literal types. There's still plenty I don't know, but I no longer reach for `any` in a panic.

**Testing** was another focus. I read a lot about testing strategies and started writing more integration tests and fewer brittle unit tests that just mirrored the implementation. It's made me more confident when refactoring.

I also dipped my toes into **Rust**. I worked through a good chunk of the Rust book and built a couple of small CLI tools. I'm not building anything serious with it yet, but it's changed how I think about ownership and memory, even in JavaScript.

## Writing and community

I wrote more blog posts this year than any year before. Not a huge number, but enough that it started to feel like a habit. Some of them were tutorials, some were about things I learned the hard way, and a couple were more personal.

What surprised me was how much I got back from writing. Readers left thoughtful comments, pointed out mistakes (thank you!), and shared their own approaches. A few posts led to conversations that turned into real friendships.

I also spent more time in online communities this year, answering questions and hanging out in Discord servers. Since in-person events were still mostly on hold for a lot of the year, those spaces kept me connected to other developers. I gave a couple of talks at virtual meetups too, which was nerve-wracking but rewarding.

## Open source

I contributed to open source more consistently this year, mostly small fixes and documentation improvements to tools I use every day. Nothing flashy, but it felt good to give back. I also learned a lot from maintainers' code reviews. There's something about getting feedback from someone who knows a project inside and out that really levels you up.

## Life outside of code

This year was a reminder that I need to take care of more than my career.

Like a lot of people, I struggled at times with the ongoing uncertainty of the pandemic. Some weeks were great. Some weeks I felt drained and unmotivated. I got better at recognizing when I needed a break and actually taking one, instead of trying to push through.

I got back into running this year, which helped a lot. It's not about speed or distance for me; it's about getting outside and clearing my head. I also spent more time reading books that had nothing to do with tech, which I'd let slip in previous years.

And of course, time with family and friends. Being able to see more people in person later in the year felt like a gift.

## What didn't go so well

It wasn't all wins. A few things I want to be honest about:

- I started several side projects and finished almost none of them. I have a graveyard of repos with a README and three commits.
- I didn't set boundaries well early in the new job and ended up working too many late nights. I've gotten better, but it took some burnout to learn that lesson.
- I said I'd start a newsletter. I did not start a newsletter.

## Looking ahead to 2022

Here's what I'm hoping to focus on next year:

- **Finish things.** Fewer side projects, but actually ship them.
- **Keep writing.** Aim for at least one post a month.
- **Go deeper with Rust.** Build something real with it.
- **Mentor someone.** I've benefited so much from people helping me that I want to pay it forward more intentionally.
- **Protect my time.** Keep healthy boundaries between work and life.

## Thanks

If you read any of my posts, commented, shared, or chatted with me online this year, thank you. The developer community has been a big part of what kept me going in 2021.

How was your year? I'd love to hear about it in the comments. Here's to a great 2022!
