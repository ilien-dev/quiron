- video: CuriosaMente episode on the origin of the Olympic Games; credits Estudio Haini and Traimelo.mx
- earliest games thought to date to 776 a.C.; religious festival for Zeus, held on a four-year cycle
- aside: "olimpiada" names the four-year interval between celebrations, not the event
- best-known origin myth: Zeus's birth, five Dáctilos (Rhea's creations, Great Mother) hold footraces for his amusement
- other deities wrestle and compete on Olympus; infant Zeus rewards winners with olive wreaths
- five Dáctilos explain a festival every fifth year, counting inclusively
- newest scientific idea from András Patay-Horváth (called a Belarusian archaeologist): games grew out of old hunting rites; only a theory but archaeological finds support it
- Greece then = many city-states, frequently fighting; a truce held at game time so athletes from all cities met at Olympia (town survives, ancient stadiums open to visit)
- ancient games ran 1179 years, continuing under Roman rule; Theodosius I ended them in 393, tied to making Christianity the imperial religion
- roughly fifteen centuries on, French baron Pierre de Coubertin brings them back; Olympic Committee created 1894
- modern debut Athens 1896, 14 nations; winners received olive branches plus silver medals
- Greeks hoped to keep them at home forever; Coubertin moved them to Paris, then other cities
- now almost every nation takes part; Winter Olympics and Paralympics were added
- first gold, silver and bronze for top three places: 1904, St. Louis, Missouri, USA
- contrast: ancient wars paused for the games, yet world wars forced three cancellations of modern editions
