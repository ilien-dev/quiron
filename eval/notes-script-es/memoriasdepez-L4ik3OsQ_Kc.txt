- subject: Blas de Lezo, Spanish admiral of the 1700s; presented as a top commander of Spain, possibly the top one
- native of Pasajes; seafaring ran in the family, wanted the ocean since boyhood
- schooling in France; enlisted in the French navy at 12, when the French were Spain's allies
- at 15, first major fight near Málaga: Franco-Spanish fleet beat an Anglo-Dutch one; cannon shot shatters his leg, cut off in emergency with no anesthetic
- once healed, in Spanish service, patrols the Mediterranean; seizes numerous English vessels, many larger than his, e.g. 70-gun "The Resolution"
- helps Peñíscola and Palermo; captives were taken to his hometown as a reward
- 1706, English naval blockade of Barcelona: floats burning straw for a smokescreen, fires incendiary ammunition at enemy ships; city survives although Spain's fleet was much weaker
- fortress of Santa Catalina in Colón: his first defense on solid ground; won, but shrapnel lodges in left eye, blind in it for life
- made lieutenant of coast guards; Roquefort 1710: forces about ten ships to yield, among them 70-gun "Stanhope", triple the size of his own vessel
- next promotions: frigate captain, then ship-of-the-line captain, age 23
- 1714, Barcelona under siege again; bullet in right forearm leaves it useless; at 25 gets the nickname "Medio Hombre" (Half-Man)
- afterward takes eleven English ships; Mallorca gives up without fighting, afraid of him
- posted in America until 1730 against pirates and English-sponsored corsairs; weds Josefa Pacheco in Lima, 1725
- returns to Spain as head of the Mediterranean naval squadron; at Genoa, arriving with six ships, extracts two million pesos and a salute to the Royal Flag
- that money pays for the Orán expedition he leads: 54 ships, 30,000 troops, city captured; Algerian pirate Bay Hassan regroups and besieges again; Lezo returns with only six ships, breaks the siege, pursues him to a fortified stronghold with 4,000 defenders
- 1734: promoted Lieutenant General of the Navy, back to America; Cartagena de Indias siege: around 3,000 men and six ships against 186 vessels, 27,400 soldiers, 2,000 cannons
- English humiliation so total their king forbade chroniclers from writing about it
- dies of plague at Cartagena, caught from English dead left unburied outside the walls
- ends on his best-known saying: every good Spaniard should urinate toward England
