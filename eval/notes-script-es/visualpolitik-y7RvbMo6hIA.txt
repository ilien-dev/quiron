- VisualPolitik episode, presenter addressed as Fonseca, about California's recurring budget deficit despite enormous wealth
- hook: as a standalone country California would rank sixth among world economies; income per head close to 70k USD
- opens with two old headlines: Jerry Brown warns of a 1.6 billion USD shortfall (2017); Stockton becomes the biggest Californian city to file for bankruptcy (2012)
- when recorded, state runs a surplus, but creator calls that mere coincidence of a strong cycle; a downturn will bring fiscal trouble back
- opinion: state accounts should be sturdier; salaries of public workers and basic services can't be switched off, e.g. no school year cancelled
- questions raised: why so strapped with top tax rates and so many millionaires; effect on ordinary residents; how grave is it
- first chapter, a Greece comparison: deficit means outlays above income, surplus is the reverse
- 2019-20 budget 209 billion USD, larger than Greek GDP; 144 billion in 2008-09; 101 billion in 1990; spending about doubled over three decades
- where money goes: bullet train linking Los Angeles to Central Valley with costs still climbing; nearly 5 billion on human and health services; 3.8 billion for children's specialized medical care; 200 million for seniors
- Texas contrast: nothing spent on these lines in a decade (jokes "cero patatero"); creator backs caring for children and elderly, issue is paying for it; same trouble as Greece and Spain
- impact depends on person: San Francisco Google employee or Hollywood actor wouldn't notice; varied economy (modern farming, tech, culture, finance, universities) means state government is small slice of GDP, less contagion than Spain; people relying on public aid do suffer
- tax design: top state income rate 13% (Hawaii second, 11%); Florida and Texas levy none; sales tax comparatively low; aim is rich carrying the load; in 2004 richest 3% supplied 60% of receipts; quip: tycoons outnumber Spanish bars
- core flaw: top earners' income swings; firms pay nothing in loss years, capital gains vanish when markets fall; Sweden by contrast has 25% VAT, triple California's, so revenue stays steady since people keep shopping
- second chapter, on homelessness: California 4th in inequality nationally; 7 of the 15 most unequal US cities; homeless count up 75% in six years, roughly 32k to 55k
- housing costs: two-bedroom rent 1,175 USD nationally vs 3,165 in San Francisco; low earner at 15k in 1980 now about 17.7k, while 100k earner now about 222k
- more causes: warm climate draws homeless from places like Chicago; Oakland shelter stays last six months and need a lottery win; shelter access NYC 95%, Seattle 60%, San Francisco about 40%
- third chapter, a "nightmare": trash, needles, rats, flies; typhus 13 cases (2008) to 167 (2018); hepatitis A, TB, staph in LA and San Francisco; opioid surge
- unrest: 2016 Berkeley protest against Trump rally, 21 arrests, 10 hurt; Berkeley and San Francisco, once intellectual debate hubs, drifting toward extremes on left and right
- 2018 exodus: 700k+ moved to Idaho, Texas, Colorado (690k left vs 500k arriving from other states); growth only from international immigration, Mexicans and Guatemalans among them
- close: calls these first-world problems yet says government must improve; asks viewers if the golden era is ending; subscribe, like, weekly uploads
