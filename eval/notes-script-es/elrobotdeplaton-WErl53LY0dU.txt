- presenter Aldo starts with a blue lobster; about one per two million is blue
- even rarer: yellow ones, and albinos
- lobsters seem not to age: metabolism stays active, they just grow nonstop; fertility holds up over time
- largest on record from Nova Scotia, 1977; said to weigh 20 kg and exceed a meter
- gag: pincers supposedly able to tear off a person's arm, then "nah", would only fracture it
- running joke, "the problem with X is...": lobsters taste great; also face predators and illness
- humans can't halt aging but can delay it: healthy diet, no tobacco, new learning daily, routine doctor visits, low stress
- life expectancy keeps climbing; researchers estimate 120-year lifespans possible by 2050
- says whoever will reach 120 is likely already born; jokes it might be him or the viewer, "nah", probably a baby from last year or today's kids
- transition: nostalgia for childhood, carefree play; Turritopsis nutricula jellyfish does something similar
- cycle: fertilized egg, larva sinks and attaches, becomes polyp, grows 80–90 tentacles, turns into medusa
- then goes backward à la Benjamin Button: body shrinks to a cyst, produces new polyps, restarts; thought endless, so biologically immortal
- repeats the gag for jellyfish: not tasty, yet predators and diseases exist
- thought experiment of a world without death: José Saramago, Portuguese Nobel laureate (1998), novel on death's intermission
- novel: death takes a holiday; chaos, insurance firms and funeral parlors collapse, hospitals packed, relatives feel useless; he strongly recommends it
- ending: Japanese researchers captured first images of humans glowing; emission about a thousand times weaker than eyes detect
- photon explained as the smallest bit of light, 300,000 km per second; we've shed them since infancy, so part of us keeps traveling through the universe, which he finds fascinating
- usual sign-off: like and share
