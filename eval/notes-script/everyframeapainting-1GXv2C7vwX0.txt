- channel: Every Frame a Painting, host Tony; episode closes out the first year of the channel
- opens with admission that one film supplied more of his borrowed ideas than any other
- the film: Orson Welles's essay film F for Fake; he treats it as a personal bible and credits it for everything he knows about editing
- today's focus: one basic structural pitfall for video essays
- pitfall = chaining beats with "and then"; a story built like that is in serious trouble
- confesses it's the mistake he commits most in his own work; demo of a repetitive "choosing to…" list that works in any order, hence dull
- fix one: the link between each beat should be "therefore" or "but", never a plain sequence
- demo clip: South Park, Cartman and Scott Tenorman, pubes-for-cash exchange, flipping on therefore/but turns
- Welles uses the same causal chaining but joins ideas, not scenes; example is the painter/forger exchange about fakes and the art market's demand
- quips that the essay's flow matches a cartoon comedy's plotting
- fix two: keep several stories running side by side; Hitchcock quoted on the point about two things going at once
- technique: let story A climb to its high point, cut to B, return to A when B peaks; abandon a thread once it goes slack
- F for Fake runs six threads: Welles, Howard Hughes, Oja, the making of the film itself, plus the forger material
- his in-head mantra while editing: banish "and then"
- conclusion: video essays are really films, so pace them as a filmmaker would; sums up with the phrase "therefore and but" and a western-style cutaway line
- notes Welles had this worked out four decades before
- closing joke: calls himself/Welles a charlatan, fakes a fresh introduction, then signs off thanking viewers with a "good evening"
