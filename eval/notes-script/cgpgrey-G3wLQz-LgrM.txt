- starts from a map dividing US counties into grey vs. colored; uses it to reopen the broader Electoral College argument
- owns up to a mistake in his 2011 EC video: measured cities by strict city limits, not the wider metro areas
- the lawyer-ish nerd side of him still wants to insist he was technically right
- why he chose city limits: metro areas are fuzzy and ridiculously huge; NYC's is 6700+ square miles over four states; having partly grown up nearby, he felt big chunks had little to do with the city itself
- looking back: weak argument, and it ignores the concern the map is getting at
- bigger takeaway: rural/urban split, not just in the US, feeds the current politics
- rejects the idea that the EC guarantees election by the states; the 11 largest states suffice to win
- that scenario grows likelier as urbanization and its politics intensify; no promise of many or geographically varied states
- "should the president represent people or states" has no right answer, just a preference: power up to federal level vs. down to the states
- personally sees empowered states as a reasonable, less divisive fit for a huge, geographically varied country, but says the EC does nothing toward that
- thought experiment: everyone moves to California, one person left per other state; the EC lets California decide alone
- could design a rule requiring a majority of states, which some mistakenly believe the EC already is; the recent spread of wins is coincidence
- calls small-state protection an illusion
- worst point: a November win can be undone in December; electors are unelected party figures, no voter chose them, many may vote freely, and their December vote is the real one
- built to let a handful of anonymous party insiders override voters or its own process; offered as shielding people from themselves
- hasn't happened in US history and shouldn't; leaving it is crazy since no citizens or states benefit
- ends by calling for abolition and a genuine conversation about what a contemporary government and voting system ought to be
