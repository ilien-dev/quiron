- opens on the "first staged photo": shows a suicide, yet really tells of an amateur inventor shoved aside by a scheming statesman and left in obscurity
- subject: Hippolyte Bayard, artist/gardener/collector/experimenter; 1839 "invented photography," with a hedge ("sort of")
- context: people had long known light could form images but couldn't make them permanent; several people cracked it near-simultaneously, each by own method after years of failed tries
- Bayard in France, tinkered in spare time while holding a day job at a government office
- method: paper dipped in light-sensitive chemicals, exposed inside a camera obscura (dark room, tiny hole); stains from leftover chemicals visible on earliest successes
- subjects: his house and garden; tools, statues, vases staged as loaded still lifes; windmills (aside: he adored them); often put himself in shot, eyes shut due to long exposure
- the famous rival: Louis Daguerre, fellow Frenchman; 1820s fame via interactive "dioramas"; by late 1830s fixed images onto metal plate = Daguerreotype
- Daguerre kept it quiet, showed few influential people who leaked to press; Bayard worked alone but wanted to share openly
- June 1839: Bayard's exhibition, first photographer ever to show work publicly; attendee quote about reality blended with dreamlike fantasy
- Arago talked Bayard into delaying his announcement until Daguerre's deal was done
- August 19, 1839: Arago explains Daguerreotype process to Academy, Daguerre seated beside him; regarded as photography's birth
- afterward: government released the process, Daguerreotypes spread explosively; Daguerre got lifelong pension and most of the credit
- Bayard demoed his process months later; too late, Academy had no interest in an alternative
- his reply: a metaphorical suicide; deadpan caption on the back — the corpse is Monsieur Bayard, government generous to Daguerre did nothing for him, so he drowned himself
- symbolism: posed nude under a shroud, echoing martyr paintings; caption says body unclaimed at morgue for days; Paris Morgue open to public daily so people could identify the dead, many suicides; theme is lack of recognition
- staged like a morgue victim with clues to identity: straw hat, plus (clearer in retake) flower vase and statue, all motifs in his other work
- afterward kept experimenting; co-founded the Société française de photographie
- takeaway: his most discussed picture; early symbolic, non-literal photo, foundation for photography as creative expression, proof photos don't always tell truth
