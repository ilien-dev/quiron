- opens ~3,500 years back in Egypt: a pharaoh dead two decades gets targeted in a strike against memory, damnatio memoriae
- statues broken, chisel used to scrub her name and image; hook asks who she was and why it happened
- reveal: Hatshepsut was female; law allowed a woman monarch but it unsettled central Egyptian beliefs
- belief one: the king was Horus (male god) alive in human form
- belief two: Maat (truth, order, justice) depended on men ruling; a female king disturbed it
- her titles as compromise: throne name Maatkare; at times "Hatshepsu" with masculine ending; not everyone persuaded
- theory: erasing her image meant to put Maat back in balance
- not the rightful heir: a regent / stand-in co-ruler; succession normally father to son
- chain: Thutmose I, then Thutmose II (her husband), then Thutmose III, who was only a young child at the time
- as senior queen and widow of Thutmose II she took over guardianship for stepson, then became a full pharaoh ruling alongside him
- suspect: Thutmose III resentful; another idea: dishonored for poor rule
- evidence points to a capable ruler: big builder, mortuary temple Djeser-Djeseru a marvel then and admired now
- economy boosted by a very successful expedition to Punt
- religious ties: said Amun, the state god, was her father
- military record: Nubian campaign; boasted of fighting beside troops
- caution: nearly all sources come from her own account, on mortuary temple walls and the red chapel for Amun
- resentment motive doubtful: vandalism came 20 years after her death, too long a gap for a rage
- alternate motive: strengthening Thutmose III's own image
- likeliest explanation: wipe out memory that a woman ever held the throne, a gender anomaly too threatening to Maat
- ending: censors were sloppy, enough evidence survived to rebuild her story
