- thesis: all viewers are swayed by every ad they've ever seen; each one nudges views of a brand or product
- today's ads aren't aimed at immediate phone-call purchases or at explaining features
- McDonalds: recognized nearly everywhere, yet keeps advertising to shape a brand image in people's heads
- two psychological approaches in use: mere exposure and classical conditioning
- mere exposure = stripped-down, minimal ads; the viewer wonders why a firm pays thousands for them
- bank queue comparison: people join the longer one, presuming a reason; the choice feels safer and simpler
- familiarity lets shoppers grab Tide over the store label, even paying a couple dollars extra
- painkillers as stronger case: Advil vs generic ibuprofen, identical ingredients (required to share the drug name), many still pay the higher price
- buyers feel uneasy about cheap generics; following the crowd is easier
- creator asks whether generics should always win; answer: not necessarily
- University of Cincinnati study: Parkinson's patients told one drug was costly name brand, other cheap; both actually saline
- the supposedly costly one produced 28% bigger motor-skill gain; placebo from higher expectations; so paying more can sometimes help
- conditioning: Russian physiologist Ivan Pavlov, 1890s; dogs drooled at his entrance, then at a bell rung with feeding
- brands link imagery and feelings to the product; the link forms subconsciously, and creator says it never fails, even if you tune ads out
- car makers are the worst offenders: ads show driving, not the vehicle; luxury = formal wear at a red carpet; SUV = outdoorsy pair, kayaks and bikes, gravel road; you purchase a lifestyle
- Beats called the biggest recent ad win: 27% share of a headphone market worth $1.8B, though sound is widely seen as mediocre
- New York Times: roughly $14 to make, retail upwards of $250
- paid celebrity and athlete endorsements from the start; price, weighty build, solid box raised expectations, probably improved perceived sound, same as brand-name drugs
