- opening gag: joke setups starting with "ever notice?" and the "what's the deal?" cliché; thesis: humor hides in ignored daily details
- purpose: practical tips for adding comedy to a story, speech or screenplay
- character first: comic types such as the know-it-all, loveable loser, bad boss, neurotic, airhead
- these stock figures originate in Commedia Dell'Arte, Italian "artists' comedy" from the late Renaissance; they still feel fresh
- Commedia method: locate a character's flaw and exaggerate it; alternatively invert expectations, e.g. brilliant person blunders, fool beats the genius
- Aristotle quote, creator hedges that he's only reputed to have said it: surprise is the key to humor
- incongruity theory: we laugh when something clashes with what we expect; examples: frog dating a pig, lizard insurance salesman, baby/nun/cat disco dancing
- running joke: baby, nun or cat doing nearly anything, especially disco
- exercise one, mind map: pick a single word (creator chooses "pickle"), jot fast associations, who eats them, childhood pickle memories
- exercise two, imagination: move from the actual to the hypothetical; coconuts replacing a horse; then drop coconuts into other famous moments from history, books, movies
- record every idea, even clichéd, obvious or silly ones; obvious to you isn't obvious to others; the flip side of the dumbest idea could be the smartest
- comedy loves irritation, frustration, humiliation; Will Rogers quote (captions spell it "Rodgers"): anything's funny when it's happening to another person
- tip: give your lousy morning to a character, and by evening it's a usable funny story
- trick: rule of three / "zig zig zag", set a pattern then break it; sample joke: rabbi, priest and coconut enter a bar
- trick: punchline belongs at the very end; same joke moved to a disco
- trick: "rule of K"; hard-k sounds sound funny (coconut, disco, pickles, crickets); creator concedes it won't always land
- wrap-up: comedy is subjective and trial and error, so keep rewriting; the most important rule is to enjoy it
- closer: Dickens quote (captions spell it "Dickins") about laughter being contagious, then a tacked-on "And disco."
