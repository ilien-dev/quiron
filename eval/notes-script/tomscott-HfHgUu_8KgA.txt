- opening: mixer tap praised as a clever invention that Britain somehow missed
- US visitors wonder why British taps resemble 1940s fixtures
- creator visiting parents this weekend; their freshly installed bathroom still got two individual taps for hot and cold
- usual theories: Britishness, stiff upper lip, tradition of never having had mixers; creator calls this only half the story
- actual cause: post-WWII house construction in Britain
- older homes: attic holds a cold storage tank, roughly 250 L
- that tank supplies the hot water cylinder, which serves central heating plus bathroom and kitchen hot taps
- tank contents questionable for drinking: poor upkeep, exposed to the elements, silt, rust; creator mentions a documented case of a pair of dead rats in one
- creator checked the regulations: water from the attic tank doesn't qualify as drinkable
- cold tap (kitchen for sure, bathroom sometimes) is fed straight off the mains; qualifies as drinkable, termed "wholesome" in the rules
- hot supply: very likely fine in practice but not legally drinkable; the two supplies are kept apart
- danger with a mixer: if a backwash valve fails and mains pressure drops, dodgy water could flow backwards and taint the wider street supply; hence not permitted
- gag: crossing the streams, a Ghostbusters reference
- now different: parents' hot tank was removed a few months back, leaving the attic empty
- shiny modern unit downstairs heats water on demand; every new build uses it, so mixers are fine
- personal quirk: childhood warning against drinking hot tap water; creator still lets cold run a few seconds at a mixer first
- closing aside: old habits are hard to shake, and it simply feels safer
