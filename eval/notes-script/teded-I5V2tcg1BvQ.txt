- opening: second-person morning in a buried lunar home; ~8 m bound thanks to weak gravity; sink greywater routed to small vegetable greenhouse; transport chute to inspect generator; 12 days of unbroken darkness outside
- framing: creator insists this is ordinary life, not a post-apocalypse setup; hooks into ESA's idea of a functioning lunar base targeted for the 2020s
- caveat: realistic version is a research outpost like Antarctic stations, not a village
- worst obstacle: cosmic radiation; moon lacks both atmosphere and magnetic field
- build plan: robots + 3D printers make covered habitats from moon soil; or take refuge in lava tubes left by ancient volcanism
- early days: supplies shipped from Earth
- farming: needs greenhouse soil and CO2-heavy air; CO2 scarce on moon but could be made from recycled material
- water: polar ice mined with a special drill reaching 2 m below surface, feeding a water treatment plant
- microbiome: beneficial bacteria and viruses for immune system must be brought in or made on site
- fitness: hours of daily exercise to keep bone and muscle; gravity is one-sixth Earth's
- why go back: Apollo covered only small regions; newer discoveries include polar ice and billions-of-years-old solar wind gas particles; more to learn about solar system history
- far-side radio telescope would be free of Earth's electromagnetic noise; silicon, aluminum, magnesium = mining economics
- biggest benefit is beyond the moon: nearest possibly habitable world is light-years away, ISS retires in about a decade; base as first step to being an interplanetary species
- Deep Space Gateway: missions departing from lunar orbit burn less fuel (lower pull), so bigger ships and more cargo; surface base doubles as test site, fuel station, supply depot
- interest from Europe, Russia, China, US; likely involves all major space agencies and private firms
- ending: in a few decades, mining, labs, tourist routes, and a construction yard beneath an orbital spaceport; we've been there, but now it could become a true extension of where humans live
