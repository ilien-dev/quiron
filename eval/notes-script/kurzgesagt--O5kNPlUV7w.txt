- video opens with a list of beautiful things: scenery, faces, art, monumental buildings, night stars, a bottle catching sunlight
- beauty is not physical; exists as a pleasant inner feeling; we call things beautiful when hue, shape, form, or proportions appeal to us
- oldest tools were shaped symmetrically; researchers found no functional explanation, so ancestors apparently just preferred teardrop forms
- standards of beauty change across history, some even reverse; golden ratio, symmetry, fractals persist in art and architecture across cultures, ancient to modern
- fractals occur in shells of snails, blooms, waves, clouds; reading them correctly was critical (will it rain? is the water safe? can I eat it?)
- natural symmetry signals normal, healthy growth; big-antlered deer = good meat, malformed wheat = risky, symmetrical face = fit, fertile partner
- beauty sense probably grew out of recognizing patterns, but has outgrown that; an instinct wired in deeply
- Alzheimer's study: patients rated paintings for beauty, retested after two weeks; same ranking although they no longer remembered the works
- creator's aside: a doubter might shrug it off as individual taste
- Mondrian and Pollock experiment: most people picked originals built on strict rules such as fractals over imitations; also picked genuine abstract works over those by kids or animals
- takeaway: hard to say what beauty is, but we recognize it instantly
- modern man-made world often favored function, cost, efficiency: concrete housing blocks, grim subway stations, shabby civic buildings, huge malls, identical dull boxes
- eye tracking: gaze stays on ornaments and details, glides over plain walls; skin sensors: large monotonous facades produce boredom, unease, elevated heart rate and stress
- 2017 hospital study (observation plus interviews): art in lounge areas left patients more comfortable and happier with their stay
- hospital with old, ugly ward and renovated one: patients in new ward used fewer painkillers, discharged roughly two days sooner; researchers surprised
- happiness study: besides health and a harmonious family, beauty of your city matters, rated above cleanliness and safety
