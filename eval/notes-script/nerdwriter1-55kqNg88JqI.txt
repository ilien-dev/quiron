- subject: close reading of Emily Dickinson's poem beginning "Tell all the truth…" (the slant-truth poem)
- opens by reciting the whole poem; creator finds it strangely straightforward for a poet known for ambiguity, unusual meter and rhyme, puzzling imagery
- irony: the poem that states her slant-truth philosophy is written in regular meter and neat rhymes, dropping the very method it praises
- first pass: one idea repeated four times — line 1 bare assertion, line 2 success via roundabout route, lines 3–4 direct truth too dazzling for weak delight, would overload us, lines 5–8 lightning image (adult explaining storms to kids; staring at it blinds)
- gag: creator starts the usual thank-you outro, cuts off — too easy, Dickinson always hides something
- second pass, line 1: "all" could mean entire truth or everybody; tiny crack that foreshadows what follows
- line 2: "lies" jumps out in a poem on truth; can mean "resides," yet "Circuit lies" also contradicts itself; observation credited to Professor Ashby Crowder
- Circuit = ordinary lived experience; Circumference = edge of what humans grasp, border with infinity / capital-T Truth
- Dickinson torn over Circumference: sometimes staying in Circuit is safer, elsewhere a letter declares Circumference her business
- creator impressed she packs that tension into four words

- lines 5–8: grammar quirk — lightning, not the children, is what gets eased

- conclusion: stranded between never seeing truth and being blinded by it; poem undermines itself and enacts its philosophy; the wobble is the lightning, Dickinson standing at the Circumference
