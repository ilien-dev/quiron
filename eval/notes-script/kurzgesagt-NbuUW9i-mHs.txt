- opens with bleak headlines: ISIS brutality, Russian invasion of Ukraine, Israel–Palestine fighting ongoing; asks if viewer feels gloomy, says don't
- thesis: numbers show armed conflict becoming unfashionable while world population is at a peak; possibly the calmest era humanity has had
- count as of Sept 2014, covering deaths since Jan 2013: 4 conflicts with 10,000+ deaths, 9 with 1,000+, 13 with 100+
- admits that's not truly calm, but no current conflict pits one country against another; all civil or local
- civil wars cause awful suffering but usually less impact than wars among nations or empires, which can field larger forces, tap full state resources and logistics, and pull in almost everyone
- first cause of the shift: colonialism's end plus the Cold War's end; collapse of communist regimes uncovered old or new tensions, often civil wars in freed states
- creator raises objection: modern multinationals in poor countries might equal colonialism; tests with Congo
- Congo: colony founded 1885 by Belgium's king, area 80 times Belgium; by 1908 roughly half the population dead from violence and exploitation, ~10 million killed or starved, millions mutilated and traumatised
- most present conflicts sit in regions foreign-ruled 60 years ago
- trend in civil wars: outright victories common through 1989, rarer now; negotiated endings rose from 10% to nearly 40%
- why states stopped fighting each other, four reasons; 1) democratisation: democracies rarely fight one another, only a minority of interstate wars since 1900 involved two democracies
- 2) globalisation: buying on world markets cheaper than seizing resources; foreigners worth more living than dead, a fairly new idea
- 4) fixed borders: after WWII countries committed to accepting existing borders and others' sovereignty, ending most territorial wars
- caveat: unknown whether anomaly or lasting peace; historical average is a couple of major wars each century; need roughly 75 more years without one to be confident
- ends upbeat: war may be finished, nasty conflicts remain but things improve; urges speaking out for peace and democracy
