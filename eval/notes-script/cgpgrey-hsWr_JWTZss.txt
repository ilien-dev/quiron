- opens on tumbleweed as Wild West shorthand for scenery; twist: they're living plants, lifecycle from seed to roots, branches, flowers, fresh seeds
- parent plant framed as self-sacrificing: starves, dies, dries out, gets blown loose, rolls to scatter offspring; mock-solemn tone, circle-of-life nod, "beautiful, really"
- main reveal: not native; no homesteaders or tumbleweed in the West before the 1800s; invasive, unwelcome; title "the Trouble with Tumbles"
- imagined viewer objection (are they really a problem?); creator half-agrees, snow comparison: small amount charming, large amount troublesome, enormous amount dangerous
- they cling to things and one another; "tumulus" wordplay; one big windstorm can bury a village under thousands, cutting off roads, vehicles, houses
- drift resembles brown snow but is thorny; rose comparison rejected; spines break off in skin or horses and fester
- clearing is infuriating: bouncy yet sticky, forked one by one; "Sisyphean"; heavy machinery risky since extremely flammable, "min-maxed kindling"; fire payoff deferred
- 1800s setup: settlers founding farms and tiny towns; feeding the young nation vital; new Department of Agriculture (DOA) wrote farming advice and gathered samples; a letter from South Dakota about a troublesome weed; creator voices South Dakota and "Grey" in a skit
- agricultural damage: stole soil nutrients, choked crops, injured draft animals, clogged or broke newly mechanized farm machinery; early estimates 20% crop loss
- DOA's education effort: "Wanted: Kill on sight" poster; aliases Prickly Thistle, Kali Tragus, Wind Witch, Russian Thistle
- Russia is the species' homeland; contaminated crop seed likely the vector, perhaps onto one farm
- Great Plains beside the Great American Desert: flat, open, windy, "tumble-topia"; South Dakota as patient zero; interior covered by 1900, then spread to Canada and Mexico
- mountains held it back until trains carried it east and west into every empty pocket
- prairie fires: firebreaks worked as tumble tunnels; fire-driven winds lifted burning tumbles across the gap like fireballs
- eradication hopeless: a third of the continent, two irritated neighboring countries, plus infestations across the other two thirds; USDA still trying from the 1800s through the 2000s, no success
