- framing: creator found nuclear-power debates annoying and confusing; video aims to untangle the subject
- starts 1940s: after wartime horror and the atomic bomb, civilian nuclear tech sold as a peaceful by-product to help rebuild
- big dreams: free electricity, Antarctic settlement, atomic cars/planes/houses; slogan that the future was atomic
- soon an "atomic age hangover": reactors turned out complicated and costly; physics-to-engineering easy on paper, tough in practice
- private companies judged it too risky an investment; favored gas, coal, oil
- reasons to keep going: exciting technology, hope of super-cheap electricity, energy independence from oil/gas imports, sometimes a covert wish for bombs
- high point early 1970s: Middle East war pushed oil prices up worldwide; investment and commercial interest jumped
- design contest: unlikely underdog light water reactor won; not innovative, not a scientist favorite, but available, working, reasonably priced
- principle: artificial chain reaction boils/heats water; fission yields millions of times a chemical reaction's energy
- U-235 hit by neutrons, absorbs one, becomes unstable, usually breaks into fast lighter fragments, extra neutrons, radiation; water heated, neutrons keep it going in a tightly controlled chain, unlike a bomb's fast runaway
- plain water is the moderator and also drives turbines anyway; handy
- creator's judgment: dominant thanks to low cost and simplicity, yet not safest, most efficient, or most elegant
- revival fizzled after about ten years: Three Mile Island, Pennsylvania, 1979, core melted, near-catastrophe; Chernobyl 1986, radioactive cloud menaced central Europe; Fukushima 2011 renewed debate and worry
- 218 new reactors connected in the 1980s; reactor numbers and nuclear's share of global electricity flat since late 1980s
- status in 2015: roughly 10% of world energy demand; 439 reactors, 31 countries; about 70 being built, mostly in fast-growing countries; 116 planned
- most reactors over 25 years old with old tech; 80%+ are light water variants
