- opens with baby photo; sarcastic setup that a cute infant must have turned out decent; alarm-sound gag
- Hitler born 1889, small Austria-Hungary town; script calls him "Adolphus"
- father Alois: illegitimate birth as Schicklgruber, adopted stepfather's surname; midlevel customs officer, modest income but plenty of romantic conquests
- Alois's first marriage: wealthy older woman; cheated almost at once, including with a far younger maid
- left ailing wife for the maid; Church forbade divorce, so he held off marrying until the wife died; had a child meanwhile
- maid became second wife, then fell sick; teenage cousin Clara brought in as nurse; Alois impregnated her after wife's death, married her afterward; creator's sarcastic "rockstar" aside
- Clara lost her first three babies in infancy, so fourth child Adolf was pampered; two more kids followed
- frequent moves, so five different primary schools
- father harsh, short-tempered, took it out mainly on the eldest son, who fled home at 14; Adolf, age seven, inherited chores and the abuse
- outcome: tense bond with father, intense attachment to a mother who fretted over his health
- early school years: strong grades, teacher praise, popular; staged war games with other kids; loved cowboy-and-Indian books
- later misbehavior: smoking, orchard raid, provoking a religion teacher loyal to Austria with pan-German symbols; running gag that every outburst from dad brought harsh punishment
- region formerly in German Confederation; locals saw themselves as German; Adolf embraced nationalism partly to defy his civil-servant father
- little brother, six, died of measles when Adolf was 10; grave in cemetery facing the house; neighbors saw him change: chatting with trees, late-night stargazing on cemetery wall; dropped religion, grades slid
- started high school, city kids treated him as a country bumpkin; repeated a grade; read and drew instead, and drew well
- scraped through last semester, celebrated by getting wasted and joking about using the diploma as toilet paper; skipped the final exam and quit school at 16
- next three years jobless, going to opera with sole friend August Kubizek; Kubizek's memoir: passionate, felt superior, hot-tempered like his father, mesmerizing when ranting
- back in Vienna with no support: shelters, selling hand-painted postcards; city's widespread antisemitism (mayor he backed, hate pamphlets, conspiracy theories) fed his race-struggle worldview, Aryans best, Jews worst; creator admits the exact origin of his ideology is unclear
- left for Munich at 24 to avoid conscription; another year as a drifter
- 1914: war erupted, crowds celebrated, he enlisted within days; war brought purpose and camaraderie; he saw it as his happiest period; reportedly brave, won Iron Cross First Class, lucky in near-misses
- 1916 shell wound to the leg; recovering at home he was angered by tired, hungry, anti-war civilians; 1918 British gas attack temporarily blinded him; heard of defeat in hospital a month later
- postwar: punishing treaty, big reparations and troop losses, national humiliation; borders redrawn, Austria-Hungary dissolved, new states emerged; he blamed communists and Jews for a "stab in the back" via unrest and pacifist agitation
