- Vsauce, host Michael; opens showing three swatches (blue, yellow, green) that anyone with typical vision would name identically
- radiation wavelength can be measured; the felt experience of color in someone else's mind can't be observed
- strawberry example: both call it "red," communicate fine, yet inner experiences might differ and nobody would ever learn it
- color blindness is a known variation; it gets diagnosed because affected people miss things the majority detect
- possible hidden differences in seeing that would leave every test score unchanged; aside: could explain why some colors are preferred or look good together, then concedes that already happens
- why it matters: each person is isolated within their own perception
- thought experiment: English-speaking alien from a distant solar system, incapable of feeling pain; learns nerve fibers (Aδ and C) to spinal cord, every cell, pathway, chemical; could ace a biology test, still never feels it
- terms: qualia = ineffable raw feelings; "Explanatory Gap" = failure to tie physical phenomena to them or to share them
- Tommy Edison, blind from birth, YouTube channel about being blind (Michael calls it amazing); in his color video, comparing red to hot and blue to cold strikes him as weird and never made him perceive any color
- Daniel Dennett: qualia may be private just because our language falls short; maybe an alien tongue could evoke color with no retina involved, or vast numbers of well-arranged words could let a blind person see a color or settle whether two friends share a red
- currently no means of confirming my red matches yours; frustrating, but that we can ask and collectively wonder is incredible and distinctly human
- animals use tools, solve problems, communicate, cooperate, show curiosity, plan ahead, seem to feel loneliness, fear, joy; apes taught sign language answer questions, express emotion, produce novel thoughts, at roughly the level of a human toddler aged two and a half
- Michael's reading: apes are plainly curious but probably lack theory of mind, awareness that others hold different knowledge
- non-asking apes parallel those small children: they don't treat others as information sources
- close: we agree chocolate is tasty but he can't enter your consciousness to taste it; can only ask; sign-off says stay human, stay curious, thanks for watching
