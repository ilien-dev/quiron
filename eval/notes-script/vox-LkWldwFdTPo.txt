- topic: South Sudan crisis; opens with UN experts' November trip to the youngest nation on earth
- UN experts' findings: mass killings, a "twisted" setting where sexual violence against women and girls is routine; UN says world must act to stop ethnic cleansing possibly rivaling Rwanda 1994 (800,000 dead)
- numbers since civil war began Dec 2013: over 50,000 dead, 2.3M uprooted, about 6M facing hunger, 70% of schools shut
- independence only five years old (2011); was meant to bring peace to a long-warring region
- background: once part of Sudan; Arabic-speaking, mostly Muslim north vs south that is mainly Christian or traditional faiths; split predates colonial era
- violence started 1950s, soon after Sudan freed from British/Egyptian rule; northerners got nearly all power, Khartoum run by narrow elite
- creator calls Khartoum regime predatory: grabbed resources, enriched insiders, neglected ordinary people
- north-south war lasted decades; 2005 deal gave south self-governance and opened door to secession referendum
- referendum January 2011, UN and international help, roughly 99% yes; US had lobbied for years
- Susan Rice, then US UN ambassador, praised it as a "day of triumph"; Obama administration cheered
- 60+ ethnic groups, biggest Dinka and Nuer; president Salva Kiir (Dinka) picked Riek Machar (Nuer) as deputy to show unity; it didn't last
- causes of breakdown: warlords and generals in jobs they weren't suited for, outside world disengaged, tensions ignored during independence push resurfaced
- Machar criticized Kiir, announced challenge in next election; loyalist forces fought in Dec 2013
- both men used ethnic militias and hate speech to rally backers; political feud turned ethnic war
- week one alone: 1,000+ dead, 100,000 displaced; other tribes joined, revenge cycles, multiple truces collapsed
- escalators: fights over resources, neighbor countries' involvement, flood of weapons
- UN urges intervention but no specific plan; US wants arms embargo, probably vetoed by China and Russia who follow the split neighbors
- timing is terrible: Obama leaving office and UN chief Ban Ki-moon finishing term, two figures with real leverage over African governments
- creator's skepticism: doubts president-elect Trump knows South Sudan exists; very unlikely he'd work to stop the killing
- ending: international criminal court created after Rwanda as deterrent; US and allies lack means to make factions disarm; independence could end in the very bloodshed it was supposed to prevent
