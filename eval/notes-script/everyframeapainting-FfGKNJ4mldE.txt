- channel: Every Frame a Painting, hosted by Tony; installment about chairs as a production design device in cinema
- opening hook: odd question about whether you've ever admired a chair in a scene, not as something to own but as a smart choice made for the story
- cold open: clip of a hotel room containing five varieties of chair, played for laughs
- thesis: in movies a chair is a design decision, and which one is picked reveals character and setting
- viewers told to switch on captions to see film titles
- setup: imagine a script of your own; what could a chair do for it
- function one (most common): chair as part of the world; audience instantly reads tidy/bright vs shabby, and broke vs wealthy; suggests life outside the frame
- examples: dinette-set line about missing seats; Game of Thrones quip about an ugly chair as Lysa Arryn's equivalent; Star Wars throne-room scene for hierarchy, plus monarchy head and ship captain
- function two (less usual): chair as part of one individual; weak, insecure man arranges seating so guests sit below him, yet his schemes get thwarted
- more traits shown: vanity, no inhibitions, a fondness for joysticks (gag)
- once Ellie is gone, Carl keeps her seat nearby, a constant reminder of his love
- function three (Tony calls it the most broadly useful): chair as part of the situation
- Godfather Part II: Fredo tries to sit upright but the chair defeats him; more he squirms, weaker he looks
- chairs alter actors' posture: some deny comfort, some allow too much (Bernie/Tom clip), some push toward theatricality (Blofeld clip)
- functions overlap; best moments start as one thing and turn into another
- closing: any prop could do this, chairs are just common and inexpensive; next time someone sits, ask what they sit on and whether it can do more
