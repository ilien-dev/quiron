- channel: Every Frame a Painting, host Tony; subject is Lynne Ramsay and film as poetry
- starts by listing stereotypes of "poetic" cinema (slow, pretentious, plotless); his definition: image and sound doing something distinctive, plot secondary
- Scorsese clip: movies he rewatched lasted because of character and unconventional storytelling; example The Wrong Man, where dread and paranoia come via camera movement plus a performer's face
- Ramsay clip: some shots are tightly controlled, she labors to nail them since details carry the scene's whole meaning
- example scene: boy provokes his mother; father hovers at the frame's margin, distracted, face withheld; later shot shows the unclean mess grown worse; Tony asks what it implies about the family
- her trick is removal, not adding clutter; attention lands on a single detail per shot
- Bresson line, as Ramsay paraphrases: if picture carries everything, drop sound; if sound carries it, drop picture; avoid overly fancy images
- cinema's special power: creating a mental state from sound and picture alone
- structure of the video: framing, then faces, then repeated details
- framing: vital information hidden from us, e.g. a woman's eyes never shown, a figure sliced by a doorway; we aren't told what to feel, so characters stay enigmatic
- faces: certain people simply look right on camera, even amateurs; she blends pros with untrained actors so they can't be told apart; wants people who show thoughts without speaking
- repetition: mother and son copy each other's posture, again a decade later; son's fingernail habit parallels mother's eggshells; film never explains, viewer links them
- case study: short film Gasman (1997); Tony hides the key twist, shows scenes from before and after, invites guessing
- Gasman details: Lynne and dad meet a stranger girl on railway tracks; they hold hands and admire her dress, framing crops their heads; later the hand-holding recurs and the girl complains of pain
- dad hoists both girls, echoing earlier lift of Lynne solo; girl returns to her mom; last image is Lynne seen from behind on the tracks
- Tony's reading: portrait of a child's mind, before versus after, through paired images; indirect, may sink in only later
- wrap-up: rare kind of film; goes small, focuses on details, shows less, finds poetry through simplicity
- final gag: if someone asks the meaning of poetry, invent an answer
