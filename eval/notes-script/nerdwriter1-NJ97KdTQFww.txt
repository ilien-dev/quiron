- topic: Nirvana's "Polly", sixth track on Nevermind; creator ranks it the most disturbing among the band's many dark songs
- quiet acoustic song narrated by the rapist; victim is a teenage girl who is raped and tortured
- hard to discuss because it's rooted in a real 1987 case: Gerald Friend, released early on parole after a comparable 1960 conviction
- claim: people instinctively look away from such cruelty and repress it; Cobain refused to
- Cobain was an outspoken feminist against sexism and sexual violence; Nirvana played a benefit for Bosnian rape victims, about $50,000 raised
- Cobain quote: that did more than complaining or zines would; use the tools you've got
- liner notes told sexists not to buy albums or attend shows; ethics over money
- thesis: his genius was artistic rather than political; music hits emotionally and widens empathy, hence the narrator choice is so jarring
- creator came late to Nirvana: about 3 when Nevermind dropped, started listening only a couple of years ago; struck by Cobain's poetry and the songs' catchy simplicity
- music: E minor, verse loop Em, G, D, C; chorus G5, Bb5, D5, E5; soft bass and high harmony vocal join at chorus
- no drums apart from one cymbal crash opening each chorus; 9-second bass break after chorus two; mostly just voice and guitar
- sparse sound gives closeness and authenticity; Cobain used a $30 pawn-shop Stella that hardly holds tune, duct tape on the tuning pegs (Guitar World, 1992); thin plucky tone strips away polish
- design idea: get Cobain inside your head, then ask what he does there; opening two lines place us in the rapist's mind
- verses: calm, amused, detached reporting, scarier for lacking emotion; vocal distant yet transfixed
- creator rejects Cobain's claim that lyrics were unimportant; journals show love of wordplay; chorus is scattered fragments of urges, remorse, pleas, cruelty, rationalizing, righteousness, ending on the animal impulse "please myself"
- verses carry you along in his thoughts, chorus submerges you; music forces us to identify emotionally with evil rather than suppress it
