- format: brief spoken parable, narrated in first person; no named people, places, products or works
- opening: narrator relaxing outdoors under sunshine, reading, close to falling asleep, when a tiny voice interrupts
- an ant has wandered onto the open page and asks the purpose of it all
- narrator's first reaction is brushing it off: explanation would be wasted on something so small
- ant answers that being tiny doesn't dull curiosity; doesn't want to die ignorant
- ant rises onto its back legs, feelers straight up, polite and keen
- narrator sighs and explains: trees are turned into paper, bundled together as a book
- books hold thoughts and carry them to distant people
- ant asks by what means; narrator replies too sharply that now isn't the time, ant's feelers sag, narrator gentles
- narrator points at the dark marks, enormous from the ant's view, and calls them words and numbers; ant doubts, narrator says they stand in for them
- ant concedes it didn't follow, yet feels a bit wiser than moments earlier; thanks narrator
- parting: ant wishes narrator well at existing in the world; narrator wishes the same back; ant slips off into the grass
- narrator reads to the page's end but keeps thinking about the ant
- narrator lifts eyes to trees, mountain, sky; moon rising at dusk, birds flying off in the distance
- narrator calls out the same question to the planet, a "green and blue sphere"; a deep voice replies from overhead
- narrator echoes the ant's words almost exactly; the voice sighs and starts the same explanation, then the piece stops
- ending: loops back on itself, narrator now occupies the ant's place; implies humans are small next to something vast
