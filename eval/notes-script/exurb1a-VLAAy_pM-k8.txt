- format: sarcastic second-person monologue addressed to a newborn, tracing a full human life chronologically
- infancy: can't walk yet, world is a trippy blur of meaningless colors, wetting yourself gets forgiven, potty training looms
- primary school: multiplication tables, alphabet, must demonstrate you've learned them
- humility: small kids believe they're the world's center; some never outgrow it; other children eventually get irritated, forcing a lesson
- secondary school: tougher exams, trigonometry and iambic pentameter with no explanation of real-world use; cram, regurgitate, forget once you leave the hall
- adolescence: strange new impulses toward classmates; craving closeness that isn't always returned, so concealment; dating, body language, sex shaping the films and books you pick for years
- university: pushed toward money because wealth supposedly equals happiness; law or maths acceptable, humanities ridiculed (Descartes-flavored philosophy-major gag, always broke)
- career: market yourself as a team-oriented people person, bury real passions (landscape gardening, music), endure about 30 years
- adulthood: finding someone to love is harder than getting clothes off; risk of boredom or the partner leaving with the milkman
- Carl Sagan name-dropped: awe is useless when friends get rich, wed or pregnant and you just want money, romance, maybe kids
- dead authors offer conflicting answers; you personally must pick a stance on God, eating meat, abortion, inherent meaning; critics call you deluded; verdict: nobody wins this contest
- old age: regret over Jennifer Smith, both 17, fields behind parents' house; he deflected her instead of saying "I love you"
- twist: no do-over; narrator wishes they'd realized this was the sole attempt
- reframe: a talking primate in space, brightest species, at history's peak, with internet, jets, smartphones as everyday sorcery
- moral: stop fretting over others' opinions; make art, do science, fall in love, avoid being a jerk
- ending: narrator admits knowing it all along but avoids dwelling; closes on "so it goes" (Vonnegut nod)
